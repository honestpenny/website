# Research: negotiating a fair car price in Alberta, with AI doing the legwork

Prepared 2026-11-25 for a planned Honest Penny guide. No site content was edited.

How to read this file:
- **CONFIRMED** means the quote was read on the primary source on 2026-11-25. Quotes from King's Printer PDFs are copied from the downloaded text.
- **PARTLY CONFIRMED** means the source supports the claim but not all of it, or the wording came from a fetch summary rather than a full read.
- **UNCONFIRMED** means no primary source was found. Treat it as popular advice, or leave it out.
- Each section separates **Official facts** from **Popular advice / commercial sources**.

## 1. All-in pricing in Alberta

### Official facts

| # | Claim | Exact wording | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | A dealer's advertised price must be the total cost. Only GST and financing costs may be left out. | ABR s.11(2)(l): every advertisement must "include in the advertised price for any vehicle the total cost of the vehicle, including, but not limited to, all fees and charges such as the cost of accessories, optional equipment physically attached to the vehicle, transportation charges and any applicable taxes or administration fees, but not including GST or costs and charges associated with financing" | https://kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Regs/1999_192.pdf | CONFIRMED |
| 1.2 | GST is the only mandatory add-on to the advertised price | "The only mandatory fee that can be added to the advertised price is 5 per cent GST." | https://www.amvic.org/consumer/your-rights/all-inpricingisthelaw/ | CONFIRMED |

### Comparison only (Ontario)
- OMVIC (Ontario) all-in pricing lets dealers exclude HST and licensing. Don't apply this to Alberta. https://www.omvic.ca/buying/your-rights/all-in-price-advertising/ (not fetched in full)

### Popular advice
- Words like "protection package", "appearance package" and "etching" are common dealer add-ons. AMVIC names nitrogen and etching specifically (1.3). Other packages aren't named.

### Timing claims (popular advice)
- End of month, quarter or model year: commonly advised. Evidence is thin and US-based. Jansen et al. (5, evidence note) show dealers push sales at month-end to hit bonuses. CONFIRMED (abstract read). Useful as evidence for "check real comparables, not the sale banner".

### Research evidence on AI-assisted negotiation (label clearly: early, simulated)
- Zhu et al., "The Automated but Risky Game: Modeling and Benchmarking Agent-to-Agent Negotiations and Transactions in Consumer Markets" (arXiv 2506.00073, 2025). In simulations, stronger AI models got better deals. The authors also found "Buyer agents sometimes offer higher prices than retail price, resulting in unnecessary overpayment." https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00073v4. MIT Technology Review coverage (2025-06-17) quotes a researcher saying the experiments "were conducted in simulated environments that may not fully capture the complexity of real-world negotiations." PARTLY CONFIRMED (search summary; the paper wasn't read in full). This supports the guide's angle: use AI for research and arithmetic, not as the negotiator.
- No peer-reviewed study was found on consumers using chatbots to negotiate real car purchases. UNCONFIRMED. Say so plainly.

### Safe uses the guide can recommend (Honest Penny's own advice, not official)
- Paste in 5–10 comparable listings (year, trim, km, price, location) and have the AI compute the median, the range and the price per km. Then check its arithmetic.
- Build a total-cost table: price + 5% GST + registration + insurance quote + fuel (NRCan rating × your km) + loan interest.
- Read a draft bill of sale or offer sheet (with personal details removed) to spot add-ons, fees and rolled-in negative equity against the ABR s.31.2 checklist.
- Draft a short, polite written offer, and practise the conversation in a role-play.
- Never paste in your SIN, driver's licence number, date of birth, credit report, banking details or the full VIN plus your name (OPC 10.1).
- Never take an AI-quoted "market price", incentive or rate as fact. Check it against live listings, the maker's site or the lender's written quote (10.2).
