# Research: budgeting section (Canada / Alberta) — 2026-11-25

Researched by: research agent, 2026-11-25. Read-only research; no site content edited.

How to read this file
- **CONFIRMED** = read on the primary/official page this session; quote is from that page.
- **CONFIRMED (secondary)** = the fact checks out across several reputable secondary sources (author pages, journals' indexes, university summaries), but I did not read the full original text.
- **UNCONFIRMED** = from memory or one non-primary source; check before publishing.
- **NOT FOUND** = I looked and found no official source.
- "Evidence" (studies) is kept apart from "Popular advice" (methods with no good trials behind them).
- Quotes are the wording WebFetch returned from the page. Canada.ca "Page details" dates are given where shown.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3.2b | Thaler & Benartzi (2004), "Save More Tomorrow," *J. ## Recommended section structure

1. **Start here (one page):** list take-home pay; track 1–2 months; split needs/wants (FCAC). "Any method you'll keep using" + "progress over perfection" (FCAC 2026 study).
2. **Your money map:** fixed, variable, and irregular (annual/seasonal) costs; turn each irregular cost into a monthly set-aside; Alberta template lines (property tax/TIPP, registration ~$102+/yr, insurance, gas heat, Christmas, back-to-school).
3. **Set it and forget it:** payday automatic transfers into separate savings (FCAC) — why it works (defaults, SMarT, partitioning), plus the honest limits of that evidence.
4. **If your pay goes up and down:** baseline month, buffer account, tax set-aside and CRA instalments ($3,000 rule).
5. **Couples:** joint/separate/hybrid; FCAC liability and death rules; CDIC joint coverage; Olson 2023 RCT with its caveats and a financial-abuse note.
6. **Cut bank fees:** fee ranges, $4 low-cost / $0 no-cost eligibility, $10 NSF cap (federal banks only).
7. **Is my money safe?:** CDIC categories; Alberta credit unions (CUDGC 100%) and ATB (Province of Alberta).
8. **Keeping it going:** check-ins, slips are normal, mental-health-aware design, choosing a tool safely.
9. **Methods compared (popular advice):** 50/30/20 (Warren & Tyagi 2005), zero-based, envelope — what each is, and that none has strong head-to-head evidence.
