# Fact check: benefits quiz and "get help" page (2026-11-25)

All pages below were fetched on 2026-11-25. Quotes are as the page reads, though some came through a page-summary tool. They are not screenshots, so re-check any number before it ships. "Not stated" means the page I read didn't say it. It does not mean the rule doesn't exist.

---

## 1. Canada Child Benefit (CCB) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-child-benefit-overview/canada-child-benefit-before-you-apply.html ; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-child-benefit/get-payments.html
- Who it's for: Parents or carers raising a child under 18 in Canada. Monthly, tax-free, and bigger for lower family incomes.
- Gates: child under 18 ("live with and care for a child who is under 18 years old"); you must be primarily responsible for the child; Canadian resident for tax purposes; citizen, PR, protected person or qualifying temporary resident (or *Indian Act*). Income-tested: yes ("As family net income levels increase, the benefit amount is reduced"). No income cutoff is stated. Must file: yes, every year, spouse too ("If you have a spouse or common-law partner, they must also file their tax return on time every year").
- How to apply: Apply once through CRA My Account, when you register the birth, or by form RC66. Then file taxes every year to keep it.
- Numbers: none checked for amounts.

## 2. Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-groceries-essentials-benefit.html ; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4210/canada-groceries-essentials-benefit.html
- Name and start: "The Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB) replaced the GST/HST credit in July 2026." Eligibility, calculation and structure didn't change. The amount rose 25% in July 2026, and the increase lasts five years (2026 to 2031).
- Who it's for: Adults 19+ (or younger with a partner or child) on low or modest incomes. Paid every three months, tax-free.
- Gates: age "at least 19 years old" in the month before a payment, or younger with a spouse or partner or as a parent living with their child; Canadian resident for tax purposes. Income-tested: yes (based on adjusted family net income). The federal threshold isn't given on RC4210. Must file: yes ("you have to file a tax return for 2025, even if you have not received income in the year").
- How to apply: Automatic. "When you file your taxes, you are automatically considered for the benefit." Newcomers use form RC151.
- Numbers (July 2026 to June 2027): "$679 if you are single", "$890 if you are married or have a common-law partner", "$234 for each child under 19 years of age". Paid on the 5th of July, October, January and April.

## 3. Alberta Child and Family Benefit (ACFB) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-child-and-family-benefit ; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/provincial-territorial-programs/province-alberta.html
- Who it's for: Alberta families with children under 18 on lower incomes, with extra money if someone in the family works.
- Gates: "be a parent of one or more children under 18"; Alberta resident; must file a tax return (yes); income-tested (yes). You must qualify for the CCB.
- How to apply: Automatic. "You are automatically considered for the ACFB when you file your annual tax return" and qualify for the CCB.
- Numbers (July 2026 to June 2027, CRA page): base amount is $1,529 for the first child and $764 for each of children 2 to 4. It is reduced once adjusted family net income passes $28,116. The working component is $782, $712, $426 and $141 for children 1 to 4. It needs family working income above $2,760 and is reduced above $47,115. Paid in August, November, February and May.

## 4. Canada Workers Benefit (CWB) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-45300-canada-workers-benefit-cwb/who-is-eligible.html ; .../how-to-claim.html
- Who it's for: Working people 19+ on low incomes, with an extra amount for people who have the Disability Tax Credit.
- Gates: "Are 19 years of age or older on December 31, or you live with your spouse or common-law partner or your child"; "Are a resident of Canada throughout the year"; must have working income (no minimum dollar amount is stated on this page); net income below the provincial limit (income test: yes). Exclusions: full-time student for more than 13 weeks (unless you have an eligible dependant), or "confined to a prison or similar institution for a period of at least 90 days". Must file: yes.
- How to apply: Claim it on your tax return (line 45300; on a paper return, attach Schedule 6). Advance payments are automatic if the CRA gets your return before November 1.
- Numbers (Alberta, 2025 tax year, the income above which you get nothing): basic amount $37,826 single and $50,232 for families; disability supplement $43,446 single and $55,852 for families.

## 5. Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) - CONFIRMED

- Note: the official name is the **Canadian** Dental Care Plan.
- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/dental/dental-care-plan/qualify.html ; https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/dental/dental-care-plan/apply.html
- Who can apply in 2026: "Applications are open for the 2026-2027 benefit period." The qualify page lists four requirements and no age group, so it's open to any eligible resident. I couldn't find this said as a separate line ("all ages"), so the wording is our reading.
- Who it's for: Canadian residents with no access to dental insurance and adjusted family net income under $90,000.
- Gates: "you cannot have access to private dental insurance or coverage" (this includes coverage through work, a pension, a professional or student group, or a plan you buy; government dental programs don't count against you); "you and your spouse or common-law-partner (if applicable) must have filed your tax returns in Canada"; "your adjusted family net income must be less than $90,000"; both partners must be Canadian residents for tax purposes. You must confirm eligibility every year.
- How to apply: Online through My Service Canada Account or the Canada.ca form, or by phone at 1-833-537-4342 (TTY 1-833-677-6262).

## 6. Disability Tax Credit (DTC) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/segments/tax-credits-deductions-persons-disabilities/disability-tax-credit/how-apply-dtc.html ; .../disability-tax-credit/eligible-dtc.html
- Who it's for: People of any age with a severe, long-lasting impairment that a medical practitioner certifies. It is also the key to other disability benefits.
- Gates: eligible "if a medical practitioner certifies that you have a severe and prolonged impairment" in one category, or significant limitations in two or more, or life-sustaining therapy. Income test: not stated. Age: not stated.
- How to apply: Use Form T2201. You fill in Part A online, by phone or on paper. "Part B can only be completed by a medical practitioner." "Both Part A and Part B must be submitted using the same method."

## 7. Canada Disability Benefit (CDB) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/disability/canada-disability-benefit.html ; .../canada-disability-benefit/eligibility.html ; .../amount.html ; .../apply.html
- Who it's for: Adults aged 18 to 64 approved for the Disability Tax Credit and on a low income. Paid monthly.
- Gates: "you must be between the ages of 18 and 64 years old"; "you must have been approved for the disability tax credit (DTC)"; "you and your spouse or common-law partner (if applicable) must have filed your 2025 federal income tax return"; Canadian resident for tax purposes. Income-tested: yes (the amount page reduces the benefit by 20 cents per dollar above the threshold).
- Start: "The first month of eligibility for the CDB was June 2025." Payments began July 2025. Back pay goes up to 24 months, but not before June 2025.
- How to apply: Online (recommended), by phone to Service Canada, in person at a Service Canada office, or on a paper form.
- Numbers (July 2026 to June 2027): "the maximum monthly benefit you could receive is $204.20". Earnings exemption: "up to $10,210" single and "$14,294" combined for couples. The income thresholds shown ($23,000 single, $32,500 couple) are from the July 2025 to June 2026 period. **The 2026-27 thresholds are UNCONFIRMED.**

## 8. RDSP: Canada Disability Savings Grant and Bond - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/disability/savings/apply.html ; .../savings/how-much.html
- Who it's for: People approved for the Disability Tax Credit, under 50, saving in an RDSP. Low-income families get government money with no deposit needed.
- Gates: DTC approved; resident of Canada; valid SIN; open the plan "before December 31 of the year they turn 59". Grants and bonds stop after "December 31 of the year you turn 49". Income test: yes (family income sets the bond amount and the grant matching rate).
- How to apply: Open an RDSP at a bank or financial institution, which applies for the grant and bond for you.
- Numbers (2026): bond "$1,000 until you reach the limit of $20,000". You get the full bond at family income of $38,237 or less, and none at $58,523 or more. "You do not need to make any contributions to your plan to receive the bond." Grant "$3,500, with a limit of $70,000 over your lifetime". It matches 3:1 on the first $500 and 2:1 on the next $1,000 if family income is $117,045 or less, otherwise 1:1 on up to $1,000.

## 9. Canada Learning Bond (CLB) - CONFIRMED

- URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/registered-education-savings-plans-resps/canada-education-savings-programs-cesp/canada-learning-bond.html
- Who it's for: Children born in 2004 or later in low-income families. Up to $2,000 for school after high school, with no savings of your own needed.
- Gates: "born in 2004 or later"; "low-income families"; money goes into the child's RESP (an RESP is needed); "Personal contributions are not required to receive the CLB"; "The beneficiary must be under the age of 21 at the time of application." ESDC search results also say the carer must have applied for the CCB and keep filing taxes. I didn't read that on the page I fetched, so treat it as unconfirmed.
- How to apply: Open an RESP for the child at a bank or financial institution and ask for the Canada Learning Bond.
- Numbers: "$500" the first eligible year plus "$100" for each later eligible year, "up to age 15, for a maximum of $2,000."

## 10. Old Age Security (OAS) and Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/eligibility.html ; https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/apply.html ; https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/guaranteed-income-supplement/eligibility.html ; .../guaranteed-income-supplement/apply.html
- Who it's for: People 65+ who have lived in Canada at least 10 years since age 18. Low-income seniors also get the GIS top-up.
- Gates: OAS: "must be 65 years or older"; "resided in Canada for at least 10 years since the age of 18"; no income test for eligibility (the clawback for high incomes wasn't checked); "You must submit a tax return each year to receive your Old Age Security pension." GIS: must "Receive the Old Age Security (OAS) pension" and "Have an annual income below the income threshold". Single, divorced or widowed: "Less than $23,112".
- How to apply: Many people are enrolled automatically; a letter arrives "around your 64th birthday". Otherwise apply online through My Service Canada Account or by paper form ISP-3550. Service Canada will "try to enroll you for the Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) automatically".

## 11. Alberta Seniors Benefit - CONFIRMED

- URL: https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-seniors-benefit
- Who it's for: Albertans 65+ who get OAS and have a low income. Monthly money from the province on top of federal pensions.
- Gates: "be 65 years of age or older"; "have lived in Alberta for at least 3 months immediately before applying"; "be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident"; must "receive the Old Age Security pension" (if you defer OAS, you're not eligible). Income-tested: yes. Tax return: the benefit is based on the previous year's return, and if you haven't filed you complete an income information form.
- How to apply: Not automatic. Apply online at sfa.alberta.ca or on the paper Seniors Financial Assistance application (call 1-877-644-9992 to get one mailed).
- Numbers (from July 1, 2026, guidelines): single "$32,690 or less"; couple combined "$53,800 or less".

## 12. Seniors Property Tax Deferral Program (Alberta) - CONFIRMED

- URL: https://www.alberta.ca/seniors-property-tax-deferral-program
- Who it's for: Alberta homeowners 65+ with at least 25% equity. You can defer property tax as a low-interest home-equity loan.
- Gates: "be 65 years of age, or older (only one spouse/partner needs to be at least 65)"; "own a residential property in Alberta and the home must be your primary residence"; "a minimum of 25% equity in your home". Income test: no ("Eligibility is not based on your income").
- How to apply: Send the Loan Application and Agreement with your tax bill online, by fax or by mail, "at least 30 days before the municipal property tax deadline".
- Numbers: "The current interest rate is 4.45%" (simple interest, reviewed in April and October).

## 13. Free tax clinics (CVITP) - CONFIRMED

- URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/community-volunteer-income-tax-program/need-a-hand-complete-your-tax-return.html
- Who it's for: People with a modest income and a simple tax situation. Volunteers will do your tax return for free.
- Gates: "a modest income and a simple tax situation". Simple means no income, or income from work, pension, benefits, RRSP, scholarships, or interest under $1,200. Clinics don't take self-employment, rental income, capital gains, bankruptcy or foreign property.
- How to apply: Find a walk-in, drop-off or appointment clinic in the CRA tax clinic directory.
- Numbers (suggested total family income; clinics may adjust): 1 person $40,000; 2 people $55,000; 3 people $60,000; 4 people $65,000; 5 people $70,000; plus $5,000 for each extra person.

## 14. Connecting Families (low-cost internet) - CONFIRMED

- URLs: https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/programs-and-initiatives/connecting-families-initiative ; .../connecting-families-initiative/eligibility
- Who it's for: Families getting the maximum Canada Child Benefit, and seniors getting most of the GIS. Invitation letter only.
- Gates: "Families are eligible if they receive the maximum amount of the Canada Child Benefit (CCB)." "Eligible seniors ... must receive at least 80% of the maximum amount of Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS)." "Only those who receive a letter can take part in the initiative." Eligibility is checked every year.
- How to apply: Enter the access code from your government letter on the registration portal, then pick a participating internet provider. The registration help line is 1-833-206-0599.
- Numbers: $10/month (10 Mbps, 100 GB) or, from April 2022, "a minimum of 200 GB of data usage for $20 a month" (50/10 Mbps). The page was last updated January 12, 2026, with no end date stated.

## 18. The penny - CONFIRMED (GI-131 itself UNCONFIRMED)

- Stop date: "The Royal Canadian Mint has ceased dispensing pennies as of February 4, 2013." Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news87/excise-gst-hst-news-no-87-winter-2013.html (archived). The Mint says the same: "While the coins remain legal tender, the Royal Canadian Mint stopped distributing pennies on February 4, 2013." Source: https://www.mint.ca/en/faq
- Legal tender: confirmed by the Mint FAQ above.
- Rounding: "cash transactions should be rounded to the nearest five-cent increment in a fair and transparent manner", applied to the final bill after GST/HST. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/federal-government-budgets/archived-budget-2012/archived-eliminating-penny-canada-s-coinage-system.html (archived). Cash only: the phase-out "will have no impact on cheque payments or electronic transactions". Amounts ending in 1, 2, 6 or 7 cents round down, and 3, 4, 8 or 9 round up. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2013/02/government-canada-royal-canadian-mint-bid-farewell-canadian-penny.html
- GI-131 ("Phasing Out of the Penny") is cited in CRA News No. 87, but the guessed canada.ca URL returned 404 and I couldn't find a live copy. **The GI-131 page URL is UNCONFIRMED.** Cite the sources above instead.

## 20. Crisis lines - CONFIRMED

- 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline: https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/mental-health-services/mental-health-get-help.html. "If you or someone you know is thinking about suicide, call or text 9-8-8." "Help is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week." It is "available to anyone in Canada." The official site is https://988.ca/ ("24/7/365 Crisis Support").
- Alberta Mental Health Helpline: https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/amh/amh.aspx. Under "Help Available 24/7": "Mental Health Helpline - Call 1-877-303-2642". Also on https://myhealth.alberta.ca/suicide/helplines-and-distress-lines: "Alberta Health Services Mental Health Help Line: 1-877-303-2642", with numbers that offer "telephone support any time day or night". Alternatives: 811 Health Link; Addiction Helpline 1-866-332-2322; 911 in an emergency.
