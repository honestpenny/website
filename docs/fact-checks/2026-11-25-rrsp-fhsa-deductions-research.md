## RRSP

### 1. Where your RRSP deduction limit comes from

**1a. The formula** — CONFIRMED
> "The Canada Revenue Agency generally calculates your RRSP deduction limit as follows: your unused RRSP deduction room at the end of the preceding year Plus The lesser of the two following items: 18% of your earned income in the previous year [;] the annual RRSP limit (for 2026, the annual limit is $33,810) That exceeds one of the following items: your pension adjustment (PA) [;] your prescribed amount for connected persons Plus your pension adjustment reversal (PAR) Minus your net past service pension adjustment (PSPA)"
Source: [LIMIT]

Plain-language note: the exact order is (lesser of 18% or the dollar max) **minus** PA, **plus** PAR, **minus** PSPA, **plus** unused room carried forward. The brief says the same thing in a different order.

**1b. Annual dollar limits** — CONFIRMED
- 2026 RRSP dollar limit: **$33,810**
- 2027 RRSP dollar limit: **$35,390**
- 2028 RRSP dollar limit: **$36,390** (also listed)
Source: [LIMITS TABLE], "RRSP dollar limit" column (2027 row: "$35,390"; 2026 row: "$33,810"; 2028 row: "$36,390").

Plain-language note: the 2026 limit is what applies to the 2026 tax year (based on 2025 earned income). The 2027 limit of $35,390 applies to the 2027 tax year.

**1c. PA is in T4 box 52 and reduces next year's limit** — CONFIRMED
> "Your PA appears in box 52 of your T4 slip, or in box 034 of your T4A slip."
> "Your PA for a year reduces your RRSP deduction limit for the following year. Your PA does not affect your income."
Source: [T4040], section "Pension adjustments (PAs)".

**1d. PAR comes from a T10 the plan sends to CRA** — CONFIRMED
> "Your plan administrator or trustee will send the CRA a copy of your T10 slip. The CRA uses that copy to increase your RRSP deduction limit for the year."
Source: [T4040], "Pension adjustment reversals (PARs)".

**1e. Where you see the limit (NOA statement, CRA account, T1028)** — CONFIRMED
> "You can find your RRSP deduction limit by going to: Form T1028, Your RRSP Information for 2026 [...] Sign in to your CRA account [;] The RRSP Deduction Limit Statement, on your latest notice of assessment or notice of reassessment"
> "Future assessments or reassessments may change the information found in the calculation of the RRSP deduction limit statement."
Source: [WHERE]

Plain-language note: CRA now says "CRA account" rather than "My Account" on most pages. T4040 calls the NOA section the "RRSP deduction limit and available contribution room statement".

### 2. Contribution deadline (the "first 60 days")

**2a. Deadline for the 2026 tax year is March 1, 2027** — CONFIRMED
> "March 1, 2027, is the deadline for contributing to your RRSP for amounts you want to deduct on your 2026 income tax and benefit return."
Source: [L20800]. Same in [T4040]: "March 1, 2027, is the deadline for contributing to an RRSP for the 2026 tax year."

**2b. The 2026 "contribution year" runs March 3, 2026 to March 1, 2027** — CONFIRMED
> "2026 [:] March 3, 2026 to December 31, 2026 [;] January 1, 2027 to March 1, 2027"
> "the contribution year is the period that: begins on the 61st day of a year [...] ends on the 60th day of the following year"
Source: [RCR-YEAR]

**2c. First-60-days contributions can go to either year** — CONFIRMED (inferred)
No single sentence says "either year", but the pieces are official:
> "For 2026, you can deduct contributions you made to your or your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSP or SPP from January 1, 1991, to March 1, 2027." ([T4040])
> "You can claim a deduction for: [...] your unused RRSP, PRPP or SPP contributions from a previous year" ([LIMIT])
So a contribution made Jan 1 to Mar 1, 2027 can be deducted on the 2026 return, or left undeducted, reported on Schedule 7, and deducted in 2027 or later.

**Error on a CRA page (do not copy):** [LIMIT] says "Contributions made to your RRSP, PRPP or SPP or your spouse's RRSP or SPP from February 3, 2025 to March 1, 2027 qualify." "February 3, 2025" looks like a CRA typo. The other CRA pages use March 3, 2026 for the start of the 2026 window. Use March 3, 2026 to March 1, 2027.

### 3. Contribution receipts, Schedule 7, line 20800

**3a. Issuers give you a receipt** — CONFIRMED
> "Registered retirement savings plan (RRSP) issuers, pooled registered pension plan (PRPP) or specified pension plan (SPP) administrators will give you a receipt for the amounts you contributed."
> "If using EFILE, show your receipt(s) to your tax preparer and keep them in case the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) asks to see them. If you are using NETFILE, keep your receipt(s) in case the CRA asks to see them."
Source: [CLAIM]

**3b. Two receipts (first 60 days vs. rest of the year)** — UNCONFIRMED as a rule
No CRA page found says issuers must send two separate receipts. What CRA does say:
- The issuer's report to CRA must split the two periods: "each record for a particular contributor to a particular plan would provide a breakdown of the total contributions made during the following periods: March 3, 2026, to December 31, 2026, and January 1, 2027, to March 1, 2027." ([RCR-YEAR])
- The receipt must show "the dates the premiums were paid" ([IC72-22], para 31).
Safe wording: "Many issuers send one receipt for March to December and another for January and February (the first 60 days)." Two receipts is common practice, not a CRA requirement.

**3c. Schedule 7 must list all contributions, including ones you are not deducting** — CONFIRMED
> "Include on these lines all contributions you made from March 3, 2026, to March 1, 2027, even if you are not deducting or designating them on your income tax and benefit return for 2026. Otherwise, the CRA may reduce or disallow your claim for these contributions on your income tax and benefit return for a future year."
Source: [T4040], "Lines 2 and 3 – Total RRSP, PRPP and SPP contributions".
Also: "You may not have to fill out Schedule 7. To find out, read the information at the top of the schedule." ([T4040])

**3d. Line 20800** — CONFIRMED
> "Deduct your contributions on line 20800 – RRSP deduction of your income tax and benefit return." ([CLAIM])
> "On line 20800, enter your RRSP deduction claim. Attach Schedule 7 if you are required to fill it out." ([L20800])

### 4. Choosing not to deduct now

**4a. Undeducted contributions carry forward and can be deducted later** — CONFIRMED
> "Unused RRSP, PRPP and SPP contributions – the amount of your RRSP, PRPP and SPP contributions that you could not deduct or have chosen not to deduct [...] This amount is carried forward to the following year and you can use it as a deduction up to your RRSP deduction limit for that year."
Source: [T4040], definitions.

**4b. "Indefinitely"** — CONFIRMED (inferred)
CRA does not use the word "indefinitely". It does say contributions made as far back as 1991 can still be deducted ("from January 1, 1991, to March 1, 2027", [T4040]), and: "Even if you can no longer contribute to your RRSP in 2026 because of your age, you can deduct your unused RRSP, PRPP and SPP contributions up to your RRSP deduction limit." ([T4040]) Safe wording: "there is no deadline to deduct them."

**4c. "When income is higher"** — not a CRA statement. It is a planning idea; frame it as general information, not CRA advice.

**4d. You must still report them (Schedule 7)** — CONFIRMED
> "If you are not deducting all the RRSP, PRPP or SPP contributions you made in 2026 and the beginning of 2027, fill out Schedule 7 [...] Fill out Schedule 7 for contributions made from March 3, 2026 to March 1, 2027."
Source: [UNUSED]

**4e. Where to see your carried-forward amount** — CONFIRMED
> "Your unused RRSP, PRPP or SPP contributions from previous years are identified on the line called 'Unused RRSP contributions previously reported and available to deduct for YEAR' on: The RRSP Deduction Limit Statement, on your latest notice of assessment or notice of reassessment [;] Form T1028"
Source: [UNUSED]

### 5. Over-contribution

**5a. $2,000 buffer, 1% per month** — CONFIRMED
> "Generally, you have to pay a tax of 1% per month on your unused contributions that exceed your RRSP deduction limit by more than $2,000."
> "Also, you can only qualify for the additional $2,000 amount if you were 18 or older at any time in 2026."
Source: [EXCESS]

**5b. Form T1-OVP, due 90 days after year end** — CONFIRMED
> "If you have to pay this 1% tax, send your completed T1-OVP, 2026 Individual Tax Return for RRSP, PRPP and SPP Excess Contributions to the CRA and pay the tax no later than 90 days after the end of the year in which you had the excess contributions."
Late penalty: "5% of your balance owing plus 1% of your balance owing for each month that your T1-OVP return is late, to a maximum of 12 months"
Source: [EXCESS]
Also note the NOA warning: "Your notice of assessment or notice of reassessment will indicate that you may have to pay a 1% tax on RRSP excess contributions if your unused RRSP, PRPP, or SPP contributions exceed your RRSP deduction limit." ([EXCESS])

**5c. Withdrawing the excess without tax withheld: Form T3012A** — CONFIRMED
> "If you meet all of the previous conditions and have not already withdrawn the unused RRSP, PRPP, or SPP contributions, you can withdraw them from your RRSP, PRPP, or SPP and not have tax withheld. To do this, fill out Form T3012A, Tax Deduction Waiver on the Refund of your Unused RRSP, PRPP, or SPP Contributions from your RRSP."
> "If you withdraw the unused contributions, you have to include them as income on your income tax and benefit return. However, you may be able to deduct an amount equal to the withdrawn contributions that you include in your income [...]"
Source: [T4040], "Withdrawing unused RRSP, PRPP, or SPP contributions".
Plain-language note: the CRA has to approve T3012A ("based on a Form T3012A that the CRA approved"). You report the withdrawal as income and claim an offsetting deduction (T4040 says on line 23200). Withdrawing does not stop the 1% tax for months already passed. Also: "You may not have to pay the 1% tax [...] if [...] you withdrew the excess amounts before the end of the month when the excess contribution was made" ([EXCESS]).

**5d. Relief** — CONFIRMED: Form RC2503 can be used to ask the CRA to waive the tax if "your excess contributions [...] arose due to a reasonable error" and "you are taking, or have taken, reasonable steps to eliminate the excess contributions" ([EXCESS]).

### 6. Spousal RRSP basics

**6a. Contributor gets the deduction, within the contributor's own limit** — CONFIRMED
> "Generally, the total amount you can deduct on line 20800 of your 2026 income tax and benefit return for contributions you make to your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSP or SPP and to your own RRSP, PRPP, or SPP cannot be more than your 2026 RRSP deduction limit." ([T4040])
> "RRSP deduction limits are not transferable, so you cannot make any contribution or claim any deduction based on your spouse's RRSP deduction limit." (Line 20800 Q&A: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-20800-rrsp-deduction/questions-answers-about-line-20800-rrsp-deduction.html)
> "the receipt should show your name as the contributor and your spouse's name or common-law partner's name as the annuitant." ([CLAIM])

**6b. Three-year attribution on withdrawals** — CONFIRMED (CRA describes it as "the year ... or either of the two preceding years")
> "If you want to ensure that you do not have to include any amount in your income when your spouse or common-law partner withdraws funds from a spousal or common-law partner RRSP [...] make sure you have not contributed to any of your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSPs in the year your spouse or common-law partner withdraws the funds, or in either of the two preceding years. Otherwise, you (the contributor) will probably have to include in your income the funds your spouse or common-law partner (the annuitant) withdraws."
Form: T2205. Source: [SPOUSAL W]
Note: [SPOUSAL W] still shows the 2025-year examples ("2023, 2024, or 2025"). T4040 2026 uses "2024, 2025, or 2026".
Also: you can contribute to a spouse's RRSP "until the end of the year they turn 71", even if you are older ([T4040]).

### 7. How the CRA knows

**7a. RRSP issuers report your contributions to the CRA** — CONFIRMED
> "You must electronically file an RRSP contribution receipt return to the CRA for RRSP contributions the annuitant or the annuitant's spouse or common-law partner made under the plan in a contribution year." ([IC72-22], para 30)
> "An issuer of a registered retirement savings plan (RRSP) has to file an information return for RRSP contributions made by the annuitant, or by the spouse or common-law partner of the annuitant under the plan in a contribution year." ([RCR])
> "The RRSP contribution receipt returns are due by May 1 of the year in which the contribution year ends" ([RCR-DUE]). For the 2026 contribution year that is May 1, 2027 (CONFIRMED (inferred) from the rule).
> "The CRA uses the information gathered from annual information returns in a variety of ways. Each year, we conduct a number of review activities that promote awareness of and compliance with the laws we administer." ([RCR])
Plain-language note: the CRA may get issuer data after you file (filing deadline is April 30; issuer return due May 1), so it can compare later. That timing point is CONFIRMED (inferred) from the two dates; CRA does not say it in one sentence.

**7b. Employers report your pension adjustment on the T4 (box 52)** — CONFIRMED (see 1c). From [T4001]: "if you report an incorrect SIN on a T4 slip that has a pension adjustment amount, the employee may receive an inaccurate registered retirement savings plan (RRSP) deduction limit statement".

**7c. T4RSP / T4RIF for withdrawals, filed by end of February** — CONFIRMED
[T4079] is titled "Filing the T4RSP and T4RIF information returns" and says: "You have to file an information return by the last day of February following the calendar year to which the information return applies."
T4040: "Report your RRSP income on line 12900 and any tax deducted (box 30 of the T4RSP slip) on line 43700".

**7d. The NOA reconciles it** — CONFIRMED (inferred). T4040: "The CRA determines your 2026 RRSP deduction limit from information on your 2025 and previous years' income tax and benefit returns, and from information the CRA keeps on record. If any of that information changes, your RRSP deduction limit may also change." CRA does not use the word "reconcile".

**7e. Group RRSP through payroll: less tax withheld at source** — CONFIRMED
> "A registered retirement savings plan (RRSP) contribution you withhold from the remuneration you pay an employee in a year automatically reduces the remuneration on which you have to deduct tax if you make the contribution on behalf of the employee. [...] However, you have to have reasonable grounds to believe that the employee can deduct the contribution for the year."
Source: [T4001], "RRSP contributions you withhold from remuneration".
Q&A: "The company I work for contributes to an RRSP for me. I get the receipt at the end of the year. Can I deduct it? [...] If you have received any contribution receipts, you may claim these as a deduction at line 20800 [...] up to your RRSP deduction limit." (Line 20800 Q&A)

**7f. T1213 for personal RRSP contributions** — CONFIRMED
> "If you have a payment arrangement contract with a financial institution to make contributions to your own RRSP or to your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSP, you can use Form T1213, Request to Reduce Tax Deductions at Source, to request authorization for your employer to reduce your tax deductions at source." ([T4040])
> "To get a letter of authority, the employee has to send Form T1213 [...] For example, if the employee regularly contributes to an RRSP in the year, they should provide documents to show the amounts they contribute." ([T4001])
Form page: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t1213.html

### 8. Refund vs. lower tax

**8a. The deduction lowers income, and so lowers tax** — CONFIRMED
> "Deductible RRSP and PRPP contributions can be used to reduce your income tax." ([L20800])
> "Claim other deductions to further reduce your net income to your taxable income. [...] Your taxable income is used to calculate how much federal and provincial or territorial tax you may have to pay." (CRA Learn about your taxes, "Completing a basic tax return": https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/educational-programs/completing-basic-return.html)
Note: line 20800 is a deduction from total income to get **net** income (line 23600), which then flows into taxable income. "Saves tax at your marginal rate" is a fair summary but CRA does not phrase it that way: CONFIRMED (inferred).

**8b. A refund only happens if you paid more tax than you owe** — CONFIRMED
> "Generally, you will get a refund if you paid more income tax than you owe." (Tax refunds: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/refunds.html)
Caveat: refundable credits can also create a refund. Safe wording: "The deduction lowers the tax you owe. You get money back only if more tax was taken off your pay during the year than you end up owing."

## FHSA

### 9. Eligibility to open

**9a. Age** — CONFIRMED (with correction)
> "You are 18 years of age or older [...] In certain provinces and territories, the legal age at which an individual can enter into a contract (which includes opening an FHSA) is 19 years old."
> "You are 71 years or younger as of December 31 of the year you open your FHSA"
Source: [FHSA OPEN]
CORRECTION: CRA's wording is "71 years or younger as of December 31 of the year you open", not "under 72". It means the same thing, but quote CRA's version.

**9b. Residency** — CONFIRMED: "You are a resident of Canada" ([FHSA OPEN]).

**9c. First-time home buyer (for opening)** — CONFIRMED
> "You did not live in a qualifying home (or what would be a qualifying home if located in Canada) as your principal place of residence that you owned or jointly owned in this calendar year or in the previous 4 calendar years."
Plus one of: "You did not live in a qualifying home [...] as your principal place of residence that your spouse or common-law partner owned or jointly owned in this calendar year or in the previous 4 calendar years" or "You do not have a spouse or common-law partner at the time you open the account".
Source: [FHSA OPEN]
Note: the test for a qualifying **withdrawal** is different (your own home only; the 30 days just before the withdrawal are excluded). See 12.

### 10. Limits

**10a. $8,000 per year, $40,000 lifetime** — CONFIRMED
> "Your FHSA participation room in the first year you open your FHSA is $8,000." ([FHSA hub])
> "The lifetime FHSA limit = $40,000" (FHSA definitions: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/definitions.html)
> "Over your lifetime, the most you can deduct from your income as an FHSA deduction is $40,000." ([FHSA DED])

**10b. Room only starts when you open an account** — CONFIRMED
> "Unlike a tax-free savings account, your FHSA participation room only starts when you officially open your first account through your financial institution." ([FHSA TIP])

**10c. Carryforward up to $8,000, so at most $16,000 in one year** — CONFIRMED
Carryforward is "in the year you open your first FHSA = $0 [;] in the subsequent years after you open your FHSA, the lesser of: a) $8,000 [...]" ([FHSA ROOM])
Worked example: Wendy opened an FHSA in June 2026, made no contributions, and her statement "showed that her FHSA participation room for 2027 was $16,000." ([FHSA ROOM])
CRA does not say "maximum $16,000" in those words: CONFIRMED (inferred) from $8,000 + up to $8,000 carryforward.

**10d. Calendar-year only, no 60-day rule** — CONFIRMED
> "The contribution period for FHSAs is from January 1 to December 31 of the same year." ([FHSA DED])
> "Contributions you make to your FHSAs during the first 60 days of the year cannot be deducted on your income tax and benefit return for the previous year, unlike contributions to an RRSP." ([FHSA DED])

**10e. Room covers all your FHSAs combined, and includes RRSP transfers** — CONFIRMED
> "the total amount you can contribute to all of your FHSAs and transfer from your RRSPs to all of your FHSAs cannot be more than your FHSA participation room for the year." ([FHSA ROOM])

**10f. Over-contributing** — CONFIRMED: 1% per month tax on the highest excess amount, reported on Form RC728 and Schedule A ([FHSA ROOM], Julie example). There is no $2,000 buffer like the RRSP one (UNCONFIRMED as an explicit statement; the formula shows none).

### 11. Deduction

**11a. Line 20805 and Schedule 15** — CONFIRMED
> "Complete Schedule 15, FHSA Contributions, Transfers and Activities, to calculate your FHSA deduction and enter the result on line 20805 of your return." ([L20805])

**11b. Deduct this year or a future year; carryforward survives closing the account** — CONFIRMED
> "The contributions you make to your FHSAs may be deductible on your income tax and benefit return for the year of the contribution or a future year." ([L20805])
> "Generally, the amounts that you contributed to your FHSAs but could not or decided not to claim as an FHSA deduction on your income tax and benefit return can be carried forward, even beyond the closure of your FHSAs." ([FHSA DED])

**11c. File Schedule 15 the year you open, even with $0 contributed** — CONFIRMED
> "You must fill out Schedule 15 - FHSA Contributions, Transfers and Activities when you file your income tax and benefit return for the year you opened your first FHSA to let the CRA know that you opened an account, even if you did not contribute to your FHSAs or transfer property from your RRSPs to your FHSAs in the year." ([FHSA DED])

**11d. RRSP-to-FHSA transfers are not deductible** — CONFIRMED: "Transfers from your RRSPs to your FHSAs are not deductible." ([FHSA hub])

### 12. Qualifying withdrawal

**12a. Conditions and Form RC725** — CONFIRMED
> "You must be a first-time home buyer for the purposes of making a withdrawal [...] This means you did not live in a qualifying home [...] as your principal place of residence that you owned or jointly owned at any time in the current calendar year before the withdrawal (except the 30 days immediately before the withdrawal) or the previous 4 calendar years"
> "You must have a written agreement to buy or build a qualifying home with the acquisition or construction completion date of the qualifying home before October 1 of the year following the date of the withdrawal"
> "You must not have acquired the qualifying home more than 30 days before making the withdrawal"
> "You must be a resident of Canada from the time that you make your first qualifying withdrawal [...] until the earlier of the acquisition of the qualifying home, or the date of your death"
> "You must occupy or intend to occupy the qualifying home as your principal place of residence within one year after buying or building it"
> "You must fill out Form RC725 Request to Make a Qualifying Withdrawal from your FHSA and give it to your FHSA issuer"
Source: [FHSA OUT]

**12b. Tax-free** — CONFIRMED
> "If you meet all of the qualifying withdrawal conditions, you can withdraw all of the property from your FHSAs tax-free." ([FHSA OUT])
> "You are responsible for making sure that all of the conditions to make a qualifying withdrawal are met. If you make a qualifying withdrawal and a condition is not met at the time of the withdrawal, your withdrawal will be treated as a taxable withdrawal." ([FHSA OUT])

**12c. Can combine with the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home** — CONFIRMED
> "You can withdraw amounts from your RRSPs under the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) and make a qualifying withdrawal from your FHSAs for the same qualifying home, as long as you meet all of the conditions at the time of each withdrawal." ([FHSA OUT])

### 13. Closing deadline and transfer to an RRSP/RRIF

**13a. Maximum participation period** — CONFIRMED (with correction)
> "Your maximum participation period begins when you open your first FHSA and ends on December 31 of the year in which the earliest of the following events occur: the 15th anniversary of opening your first FHSA [;] you turn 71 years of age [;] the year following your first qualifying withdrawal from your FHSA"
Source: [FHSA CLOSE]
CORRECTION to the brief: it is December 31 of the year of the **15th anniversary** of opening (CRA example: opened August 2025, period "will end on December 31, 2040"). Not "end of the 15th year after opening".
Error on a CRA page (do not copy): the Pedro example in [FHSA CLOSE] says he withdrew in 2032, then says the period ends "December 31, 2032, because he made his first qualifying withdrawal in 2031". The example years do not match each other. Rely on the rule text, not the example.

**13b. What happens if you don't close** — CONFIRMED
> "If property remains in your accounts after your maximum participation period ends, they lose their status as FHSAs. You must then include the fair market value (FMV) of all of the property in your FHSAs as of the end of the day on December 31 of that year as income [...] Your FHSA issuer will give you a T4FHSA slip showing the amount deemed received on FHSA cessation in box 26." ([FHSA CLOSE])

**13c. Tax-free transfer to RRSP/RRIF without using RRSP room** — CONFIRMED
> "You will be allowed to transfer property from your FHSAs to your RRSPs or RRIFs without any immediate tax consequences, as long as it is a direct transfer and you do not have an excess FHSA amount."
> "Generally, an amount that is transferred directly from your FHSAs to your RRSPs or RRIFs will not impact your unused RRSP deduction room or your unused FHSA participation room."
> "If you do not do a direct transfer, the amount you withdraw from your FHSAs would be a taxable withdrawal and would be treated as a new RRSP contribution."
Source: [FHSA OUT]
Note: money moved into the RRSP is taxed when it is later withdrawn, like any RRSP money ([FHSA OUT]).

### 14. Transfers from RRSP to FHSA

CONFIRMED, all from [FHSA IN]:
> "Transfers from your RRSPs to your FHSAs must not exceed your unused FHSA participation room at the time of the transfer."
> "If you make a direct transfer from your RRSPs to your FHSAs, the transfer will reduce your unused FHSA participation room."
> "The transfer of property from your RRSPs to your FHSAs will not restore your unused RRSP deduction room."
> "any transfers from your RRSPs to your FHSAs are not deductible."
> It must be a direct transfer: "you must not withdraw the property yourself and contribute it to another one of your plans or accounts."
They also count toward the $40,000 lifetime limit ("Transfers that you make from your RRSPs to your FHSAs will reduce the amount that can be deducted over your lifetime", [FHSA DED]).

**15a. Issuers send T4FHSA slips; the CRA gets a copy** — CONFIRMED

### 16. TFSA room in your CRA account can be out of date

CONFIRMED. All quotes from [TFSA ROOM] (page date 2026-02-20):
> "Use your own financial records to calculate your available contribution room, not the information in your CRA account."
> "The TFSA information in your CRA account is only updated once per year in the spring with your transactions of the previous year. This update occurs after your TFSA issuer reports your transactions to us for that year."
> "When you contribute to your TFSA, it reduces your available contribution room immediately by the same amount that you contributed. (However, this is not immediately updated in your CRA account.)"
> "Financial institutions only send transaction information for a given calendar year at the end of February of the following year."
> "TFSA records from 2025 will be processed by April 2026. This is the best time to check your TFSA contribution room in your CRA account. Always verify your contribution room with your financial institution records to avoid over-contribution."

Plain-language note: the CRA figure can be missing the previous year's transactions until about April, and it never includes the current year's. TFSA 2026 dollar limit: $7,000 ([LIMITS TABLE]). The table lists no 2027 TFSA limit yet.

## Summary of what is NOT confirmed or needs care

1. **Two receipts (Jan–Feb vs. Mar–Dec)**: common practice, not a CRA rule. CRA only requires the issuer's report to CRA to split the two periods and the receipt to show the dates paid.
2. **"Indefinitely"** (RRSP carryforward): CRA doesn't use the word. Say "no deadline to deduct them".
3. **"Saves tax at your marginal rate"**: fair, but not CRA wording. Line 20800 reduces net income (line 23600).
4. **FHSA room lag like the TFSA one**: not found. CRA's FHSA caution is about unassessed returns.
5. **No $2,000 FHSA over-contribution buffer**: implied by the formula, not stated directly.
6. **CRA page errors to avoid copying**: [LIMIT] "February 3, 2025 to March 1, 2027" (should be March 3, 2026); [FHSA CLOSE] Pedro example years don't match each other; [SPOUSAL W] still shows 2025-year examples.
7. **Corrections to the brief**: FHSA age is "71 or younger as of Dec 31 of the year you open"; the FHSA closing deadline is Dec 31 of the year of the 15th anniversary; RC4178 is a RRIF-death guide, not an FHSA guide.
