Status key:
- **CONFIRMED**: exact wording found on an official page today.
- **CONFIRMED (inferred)**: the official text supports it, but not in the exact words of the brief.
- **UNCONFIRMED**: no official wording could be read today. Do not publish as fact.
- **CORRECTION**: the brief's wording differs from the official source.
- **PROPOSED**: in a bill that is not law yet. Must be labelled "proposed" on the page.

## 1. Which flights the APPR covers

1.1 **Claim:** The rules cover flights to, from and within Canada, including connecting flights.
- Quote [CTA ACT s. 86.11(1)]: "The Agency shall, after consulting with the Minister, make regulations in relation to flights to, from and within Canada, including connecting flights,"
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

1.2 **Claim:** The rules apply to Canadian and foreign airlines alike.
- Quote [APPR s. 2(1)]: "The carrier operating a flight is liable to passengers with respect to the obligations set out in sections 7 to 22 and 24, or, if they are more favourable to those passengers, the obligations on the same matter that are set out in the applicable tariff."
- Quote [APPR s. 7(1)]: "A carrier operating a flight to or from an airport in Canada must display ... a notice"
- Status: **CONFIRMED (inferred)**. The law says "carrier" and "flights to, from and within Canada" with no nationality limit. No CTA page read today says "foreign airlines" in those words. Safe wording: "The rules apply to any airline flying to, from or within Canada."

1.3 **Claim:** The airline actually flying the plane is the one responsible (code-share).
- Quote [APPR s. 2(1)]: "The carrier operating a flight is liable to passengers" (see 1.2).
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

1.4 **Claim:** Charter flights are covered if seats were sold to the public (and, for international, the trip began in Canada).
- Quote [APPR s. 4(1)]: "(a) to a charter flight within Canada if one or more seats on that flight are purchased for resale to the public; or (b) to a charter flight to or from Canada if one or more passengers began their itinerary in Canada and one or more seats on that flight are purchased for resale to the public."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

1.5 **Claim:** You can't be paid twice for the same event under two passenger-rights regimes (e.g. APPR and EU261).
- Quote [CTA ACT s. 86.11(3)]: "A person shall not receive compensation from a carrier under regulations made under subsection (1) if that person has already received compensation for the same event under a different passenger rights regime than the one provided for under this Act."
- Quote [APPR s. 3(3)]: "a passenger must not be refused compensation in accordance with these Regulations for an event on the grounds that they are also eligible for compensation for the same event under a different passenger rights regime."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

1.6 **Claim:** Large vs small airline.
- Quote [APPR s. 1(2)]: "large carrier means a carrier that has transported a worldwide total of two million passengers or more during each of the two preceding calendar years." / "small carrier means any carrier that is not a large carrier."
- Quote [CTA-SIZE]: "Large airlines have carried more than 2,000,000 passengers in each of the past 2 calendar years." The page names as large: Air Canada (including Jazz and Rouge), WestJet, Sunwing Airlines, Air Transat, Porter Airlines and Flair Airlines; and says "Currently, Canada Jetlines, Canadian North, and other Canadian airlines are considered small carriers."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Note: the regulation says "two million ... or more"; the CTA page says "more than 2,000,000". Use the regulation's wording. The CTA's list may be out of date (it still names Sunwing and Canada Jetlines), so don't reproduce the list as current; link to the page.
- Small airline flying for a large one: [APPR s. 1(4)] "a small carrier has the same obligations as a large carrier towards a passenger that it carries on behalf of a large carrier under a commercial agreement with that carrier." **CONFIRMED.**

## 2. The three categories and what each one triggers

2.1 **Outside the airline's control** (APPR s. 10). Full list, verbatim [APPR s. 10(1)]:
"This section applies to a carrier when there is delay, cancellation or denial of boarding due to situations outside the carrier's control, including but not limited to the following:
(a) war or political instability;
(b) illegal acts or sabotage;
(c) meteorological conditions or natural disasters that make the safe operation of the aircraft impossible;
(d) instructions from air traffic control;
(e) a NOTAM, as defined in subsection 101.01(1) of the Canadian Aviation Regulations;
(f) a security threat;
(g) airport operation issues;
(h) a medical emergency;
(i) a collision with wildlife;
(j) a labour disruption within the carrier or within an essential service provider such as an airport or an air navigation service provider;
(k) a manufacturing defect in an aircraft that reduces the safety of passengers and that was identified by the manufacturer of the aircraft concerned, or by a competent authority; and
(l) an order or instruction from an official of a state or a law enforcement agency or from a person responsible for airport security."
- Knock-on delays [APPR s. 10(2)]: "A delay, cancellation or denial of boarding that is directly attributable to an earlier delay or cancellation that is due to situations outside the carrier's control, is considered to also be due to situations outside that carrier's control if that carrier took all reasonable measures to mitigate the impact of the earlier flight delay or cancellation."
- What it triggers [APPR s. 10(3)]: information (s. 13); "in the case of a delay of three hours or more, provide alternate travel arrangements or a refund, in the manner set out in section 18, to a passenger who desires such arrangements"; for a cancellation, "provide alternate travel arrangements or a refund, in the manner set out in section 18"; for denied boarding, alternate travel arrangements. No compensation, no meals or hotel required under the APPR.
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Note: a strike at the airline itself is on the "outside control" list (j). Weather counts only when it makes "the safe operation of the aircraft impossible" (c).

2.2 **Within the airline's control but required for safety** (APPR s. 11).
- Definition [APPR s. 1(1)]: "required for safety purposes means required by law in order to reduce risk to passenger safety and includes required by safety decisions made within the authority of the pilot of the aircraft or any decision made in accordance with a safety management system ... but does not include scheduled maintenance in compliance with legal requirements."
- Definition [APPR s. 1(1)]: "mechanical malfunction means a mechanical problem that reduces the safety of passengers but does not include a problem that is identified further to scheduled maintenance undertaken in compliance with legal requirements."
- What it triggers [APPR s. 11(3)-(5)]: information (s. 13); standards of treatment (s. 14) "if a passenger is informed of the delay less than 12 hours before the departure time that is indicated on their original ticket"; for delays of three hours or more, cancellations and denied boarding, "alternate travel arrangements or a refund, in the manner set out in section 17". No compensation for inconvenience.
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

2.3 **Within the airline's control** (APPR s. 12): everything that is neither s. 10 nor s. 11.
- Quote [APPR s. 12(1)]: "this section applies to a carrier when there is delay, cancellation or denial of boarding that is within the carrier's control but is not referred to in subsections 11(1) or (2)."
- Triggers [APPR s. 12(2)-(4)]: information; standards of treatment if told less than 12 hours before departure; rebooking or refund under s. 17; and compensation under s. 19 "if a passenger is informed 14 days or less before the departure time on their original ticket that the arrival of their flight at the destination that is indicated on that original ticket will be delayed" (delay) or "if a passenger is informed of the cancellation 14 days or less before the departure time" (cancellation). Denied boarding: compensation under s. 20.
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

2.4 **Claim:** Crew shortages and mechanical problems are generally within the airline's control.
- The APPR has no list of "within control" examples. What the official text supports:
  - Scheduled maintenance is excluded from "required for safety" [APPR s. 1(1), quoted in 2.2], so delays caused by planned maintenance fall in the plain "within control" category.
  - A mechanical problem that reduces safety is normally "within control but required for safety" (the Act itself names it: [CTA ACT s. 86.11(1)(b)(ii)] "when the delay, cancellation or denial of boarding is within the carrier's control, but is required for safety purposes, including in situations of mechanical malfunctions"). That means rebooking/refund and care, but **no compensation**, unless it is a manufacturer-identified defect, which is "outside control" (s. 10(1)(k)).
  - [CTA-CONTROL] lists "staffing schedules" and "planned aircraft maintenance" among decisions "made during regular operations" (within control), and "aircraft defects identified by manufacturers or safety authorities" and "severe weather or natural disasters" as outside control.
- Status: **CONFIRMED (inferred)** for crew/staffing being within control (the CTA says "staffing schedules", not "crew shortages"). **CORRECTION** for "mechanical problems are within control" as a blanket statement: safety-related mechanical malfunctions are "within control but required for safety", which does **not** pay compensation. Suggested wording: "Crew scheduling and planned maintenance are within the airline's control. A safety-related mechanical fault is usually 'within control but required for safety': you get rebooking or a refund and care, but not the cash compensation."

2.5 **Claim:** The airline has to prove the cause (burden of proof).
- Quote [CTA ACT s. 85.07(2)], in force now: "If a complaint raises an issue as to whether a flight delay, flight cancellation or denial of boarding is within a carrier's control, is within a carrier's control but is required for safety reasons or is outside a carrier's control, it is presumed to be within the carrier's control and not required for safety reasons unless the carrier proves the contrary."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. This is already law (added by S.C. 2023, c. 26), not a Bill C-31 proposal. It applies in the CTA complaint process. Separately, the Act lists "AMENDMENTS NOT IN FORCE" from 2023, c. 26, s. 460 that would replace the three categories with "exceptions" that are "presumed not to apply unless the carrier proves the contrary". Those are **not in force**; the [LOP] summary confirms they "have not yet come into force".

## 3. Compensation for delays and cancellations within the airline's control

3.1 **Claim:** Large airlines $400 (3 to under 6 h), $700 (6 to under 9 h), $1,000 (9 h+); small airlines $125 / $250 / $500. Delay is measured by arrival at the destination on the original ticket.
- Quote [APPR s. 19(1)]:
  "(a) in the case of a large carrier,
  (i) $400, if the arrival of the passenger's flight at the destination that is indicated on the original ticket is delayed by three hours or more, but less than six hours,
  (ii) $700, if the arrival of the passenger's flight at the destination that is indicated on the original ticket is delayed by six hours or more, but less than nine hours, or
  (iii) $1,000, if the arrival of the passenger's flight at the destination that is indicated on the original ticket is delayed by nine hours or more; and
  (b) in the case of a small carrier,
  (i) $125, ... three hours or more, but less than six hours,
  (ii) $250, ... six hours or more, but less than nine hours, or
  (iii) $500, ... nine hours or more."
- "Arrival" defined [APPR s. 1(2)]: "arrival means the time when one of the doors of an aircraft is opened after it lands to allow passengers to leave the aircraft."
- [CTA-DELAY]: "you arrived late at your final destination by 3 hours or more"
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Note the bands are "three hours or more, but less than six hours" and so on (exactly 6:00 late = $700).

3.2 **Claim:** Only if you were told 14 days or less before departure.
- Quote: see 2.3; [CTA-DELAY]: "you were informed of the delay or cancellation 14 days or less before your original departure time"
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

3.3 **Claim:** If you take a refund instead of travelling, minimum compensation is $400 (large) / $125 (small).
- Quote [APPR s. 19(2)]: "if paragraph 12(2)(d) or (3)(d) applies to a carrier and the passenger's ticket is refunded in accordance with subsection 17(2), the carrier must provide a minimum compensation of (a) $400, in the case of a large carrier; and (b) $125, in the case of a small carrier."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

3.4 **Claim:** You have 1 year to claim; the airline has 30 days to respond.
- Quote [APPR s. 19(3)]: "a passenger must file a request for compensation with the carrier before the first anniversary of the day on which the flight delay or flight cancellation occurred."
- Quote [APPR s. 19(4)]: "The carrier must, within 30 days after the day on which it receives the request, provide the compensation or an explanation as to why compensation is not payable."
- [CTA-DEADLINES]: "File a request for compensation with the airline within 1 year of the delay or cancellation." / "The airline has 30 days to make a payment or explain why not."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Note: 30 days to pay **or explain**, not 30 days to pay.

3.5 **Claim:** Compensation must be money unless you choose otherwise in writing.
- Quote [APPR s. 21]: "A carrier who is required to provide compensation must do so in the form of money, unless (a) it offers compensation in another form that has a greater monetary value than the minimum monetary value ...; (b) the passenger has been informed in writing of the monetary value of the other form of compensation; (c) the other form of compensation does not expire; and (d) the passenger confirms in writing that they have been informed of their right to receive monetary compensation and have chosen the other form of compensation."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

## 4. Rebooking and refunds

4.1 **Within control (including safety):** large airline must rebook on its own or a partner's next flight leaving within 9 hours; failing that, any airline within 48 hours; failing that, from a nearby airport. Small airline: own or partner's next available flight.
- Quote [APPR s. 17(1)(a)(i)]: "a confirmed reservation for the next available flight that is operated by the original carrier, or a carrier with which the original carrier has a commercial agreement, ... and departs within nine hours of the departure time that is indicated on that original ticket,"
- Quote [APPR s. 17(1)(a)(ii)]: "a confirmed reservation for a flight that is operated by any carrier ... and departs within 48 hours of the departure time that is indicated on that original ticket if the carrier cannot provide a confirmed reservation that complies with subparagraph (i)"
- Refund [APPR s. 17(2)]: "If the alternate travel arrangements offered in accordance with subsection (1) do not accommodate the passenger's travel needs, the carrier must (a) if the passenger is no longer at the point of origin ... and the travel no longer serves a purpose ..., refund the ticket and provide ... a confirmed reservation for a flight to that point of origin ...; and (b) in any other case, refund the unused portion of the ticket."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

4.2 **Outside control:** rebook within 48 hours; if the airline can't, you choose a refund or other rebooking. (From the 2022-09-08 amendment, SOR/2022-134.)
- Quote [APPR s. 18(1)]: "a confirmed reservation for the next available flight that is operated by the original carrier, or a carrier with which the original carrier has a commercial agreement, ... and departs within 48 hours of the departure time that is indicated on that ticket."
- Quote [APPR s. 18(1.1)]: "If a carrier cannot provide a confirmed reservation in accordance with subsection (1), it must, at the passenger's choice, refund any unused portion of the ticket or provide the following alternate travel arrangements, free of charge:"
- Quote [APPR s. 18(1.3)]: "A passenger who is eligible to be refunded under subsection (1.1) may choose a refund at any time prior to being provided with a confirmed reservation."
- Amendment: s. 18 is annotated "SOR/2022-134, s. 5"; the regulations are "last amended on 2022-09-08".
- Status: **CONFIRMED** (the 2022-09-08 date is the "last amended" date on the page; that it is the in-force date of SOR/2022-134 is **CONFIRMED (inferred)**).

4.3 **Claim:** Refunds go back the way you paid, within 30 days; vouchers only if you agree in writing and they don't expire.
- Quote [APPR s. 18.2(1)]: "All refunds provided under these Regulations must be paid to the person who purchased the ticket or additional service and must be paid using the method used for the original payment, unless (a) the person has been informed in writing of the monetary value ...; (b) the refund is offered in another form that does not expire; and (c) the person confirms, in writing, that they have been informed of their right to receive the refund by the method used for the original payment and have chosen to receive the refund in another form."
- Quote [APPR s. 18.2(2)]: "Refunds must be provided by a carrier within 30 days after the day on which the carrier becomes obligated to provide the refund."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

4.4 **Claim:** Unused extras (seat selection, bags) are refunded; downgrade is refunded; upgrade is free.
- Quote [APPR s. 18.1(1)]: "A carrier must refund the cost of any additional services purchased in connection with a passenger's original ticket if the passenger has been provided with alternate travel arrangements under section 17 or 18 and (a) the passenger did not receive those services; or (b) those services were paid for a second time."
- Quote [APPR s. 18.1(2)]: "If the alternate travel arrangements provide for a lower class of service than the original ticket, the carrier must refund the difference in the cost of the applicable portion of the ticket."
- Quote [APPR s. 17(5) / 18(3)]: "If the alternate travel arrangements provide for a higher class of service than the original ticket, the carrier must not request supplementary payment."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

## 5. Standards of treatment (food, drink, hotel) and communication

5.1 **Claim:** After 2 hours past the ticketed departure: food, drink and a means of communication. Overnight: hotel and transport. Applies only to within-control (including safety) disruptions when told less than 12 hours before departure.
- Quote [APPR s. 14(1)]: "If paragraph 11(3)(b) or (4)(b) or 12(2)(b) or (3)(b) applies to a carrier, and a passenger has waited two hours after the departure time that is indicated on their original ticket, the carrier must provide the passenger with the following treatment free of charge: (a) food and drink in reasonable quantities, taking into account the length of the wait, the time of day and the location of the passenger; and (b) access to a means of communication."
- Quote [APPR s. 14(2)]: "... the carrier expects that the passenger will be required to wait overnight ..., the air carrier must offer, free of charge, hotel or other comparable accommodation that is reasonable in relation to the location of the passenger, as well as transportation to the hotel or other accommodation and back to the airport."
- Quote [APPR s. 14(3)]: "The carrier may limit or refuse to provide a standard of treatment referred to in subsection (1) or (2) if providing that treatment would further delay the passenger."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Not owed for outside-control disruptions under the APPR (s. 10(3) does not reference s. 14).

5.2 **Claim:** The airline must tell you the reason, what you may be owed and your recourse, with updates every 30 minutes.
- Quote [APPR s. 13(1)]: "(a) the reason for the delay, cancellation or denial of boarding; (b) the compensation to which the passenger may be entitled for the inconvenience; (c) the standard of treatment for passengers, if any; and (d) the recourse available against the carrier, including their recourse to the Agency."
- Quote [APPR s. 13(2)]: "In the case of a delay, the carrier must communicate status updates to passengers every 30 minutes until a new departure time for the flight is set or alternate travel arrangements have been made for the affected passenger."
- Quote [APPR s. 13(3)]: "The carrier must communicate to passengers any new information as soon as feasible."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Applies in all three categories.

5.3 **Claim:** The required notice says "delayed for at least two hours".
- Quote [APPR s. 5(5) / 7(1)]: "If you are denied boarding, your flight is cancelled or delayed for at least two hours, or your baggage is lost or damaged, you may be entitled to certain standards of treatment and compensation under the Air Passenger Protection Regulations."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

## 6. Denied boarding (bumping)

6.1 **Claim:** $900 (arrival under 6 h late), $1,800 (6 to under 9 h), $2,400 (9 h+), paid within 48 hours.
- Quote [APPR s. 20(1)]: "(a) $900, if the arrival of the passenger's flight at the destination that is indicated on the original ticket is delayed by less than six hours; (b) $1,800, ... six hours or more, but less than nine hours; and (c) $2,400, ... nine hours or more."
- Quote [APPR s. 20(2)]: "The carrier must provide the compensation to the passenger as soon as it is operationally feasible, but not later than 48 hours after the time of the denial of boarding."
- Quote [APPR s. 20(4)]: "If it is not possible to provide the compensation before the boarding time of the flight reserved as part of alternate travel arrangements, the carrier must provide the passenger with a written confirmation of the amount of the compensation that is owed."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Same amounts for large and small airlines. Only for within-control denied boarding (s. 12(4)(d)).

6.2 **Claim:** Volunteers first; no bumping once on board.
- Quote [APPR s. 15(1)]: "it must not deny boarding to a passenger unless it has asked all passengers if they are willing to give up their seat."
- Quote [APPR s. 15(2)]: "The carrier must not deny boarding to a passenger who is already on board the aircraft, unless the denial of boarding is required for reasons of safety."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**.

## 7. Lost, delayed or damaged baggage

7.1 **Claim:** Liability follows the Montreal Convention; baggage counts as lost after 21 days; bag fees are refunded.
- Quote [APPR s. 23(1)]: "If a carrier admits to the loss of baggage, or if baggage is lost for more than 21 days or is damaged, the carrier must provide compensation equal to or greater than the sum of (a) the fees paid for that baggage, (b) in cases where the Carriage by Air Act applies, the compensation payable in accordance with that Act, and (c) in cases where the Carriage by Air Act does not apply, the amount that would be payable by the carrier in accordance with the Convention ..."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. Domestic flights get Montreal Convention-level compensation through s. 23(1)(c).

7.2 **Claim:** Limit is 1,519 SDR (about $2,780).
- Quote [CTA-BAG]: "The maximum compensation amount is 1,519 Special Drawing Rights (SDR), which is approximately $2,780 CAD." (for flights as of December 28, 2024)
- Statute text [CBAA Sch. VI, Art. 22(2)] still prints the original "limited to 1 000 Special Drawing Rights for each passenger"; the Convention's limits are revised by ICAO every five years (Art. 24), so the printed figure is out of date.
- Status: **CONFIRMED** from the CTA page. The dollar figure moves with exchange rates; say "about".

7.3 **Claim:** Claim time limits: damage 7 days, delay 21 days, written.
- Quote [CBAA Sch. VI, Art. 31(2)]: "In the case of damage, the person entitled to delivery must complain to the carrier forthwith after the discovery of the damage, and, at the latest, within seven days from the date of receipt in the case of checked baggage ... In the case of delay, the complaint must be made at the latest within twenty-one days from the date on which the baggage or cargo have been placed at his or her disposal."
- Quote [CBAA Art. 31(3)-(4)]: "Every complaint must be made in writing ..." / "If no complaint is made within the times aforesaid, no action shall lie against the carrier, save in the case of fraud on its part."
- [CTA-BAG]: damaged "Within 7 days of receiving your baggage"; delayed (international) "Within 21 days of receiving your baggage"; delayed (domestic) "As soon as possible and no later than the time limit found in the airline's tariff"; lost "As soon as possible".
- Status: **CONFIRMED**, with a **CORRECTION** to the brief: for **domestic** flights the CTA says the delayed-bag deadline is the one in the airline's tariff, not a flat 21 days.

7.4 **Claim:** Two-year limit to sue under the Montreal Convention.
- Quote [CBAA Art. 35(1)]: "The right to damages shall be extinguished if an action is not brought within a period of two years, reckoned from the date of arrival at the destination ..."
- Status: **CONFIRMED** (optional for the guide).

## 8. Tarmac delays

8.1 **Claim:** Chance to get off after 3 hours, unless take-off is likely within 3 hours 45 minutes; lavatories, air, food and drink throughout.
- Quote [APPR s. 9(1)]: "If a flight is delayed on the tarmac at an airport in Canada, the carrier must provide an opportunity for passengers to disembark (a) three hours after the aircraft doors have been closed for take-off; and (b) three hours after the flight has landed, or at any earlier time if it is feasible."
- Quote [APPR s. 9(2)]: "However, a carrier is not required to provide an opportunity for passengers to disembark if it is likely that take-off will occur less than three hours and 45 minutes after the doors of the aircraft are closed for take-off or after the flight has landed and the carrier is able to continue to provide the standard of treatment referred to in section 8."
- Quote [APPR s. 8(1)]: "(a) if the aircraft is equipped with lavatories, access to those lavatories in working order; (b) proper ventilation and cooling or heating of the aircraft; (c) if it is feasible to communicate with people outside of the aircraft, the means to do so; and (d) food and drink, in reasonable quantities ..."

## 12. EU261 for flights from the EU

12.1 **Claim:** EU rules apply to flights leaving the EU on any airline, and to flights into the EU on an EU airline.
- [EU] "EU air passenger rights apply: If your flight is within the EU and is operated either by an EU or a non-EU airline / If your flight arrives in the EU from outside the EU and is operated by an EU airline / If your flight departs from the EU to a non-EU country operated by an EU or a non-EU airline / If you have not already received benefits (compensation, re-routing, assistance from the airline) for flight related problems for this journey under the relevant law of a non-EU country."
- Status: **CONFIRMED**. So Paris to Toronto on Air Canada: EU261 applies. Toronto to Paris on Air Canada: no EU261 (APPR applies). Toronto to Paris on Air France: EU261 applies.

## Corrections to the brief
- Mechanical problems are not simply "within control": a safety-related mechanical malfunction is "within control but required for safety", which gives rebooking/refund and care but no compensation. Planned maintenance and staffing are within control.
- Burden of proof is already law (s. 85.07(2) of the Act), not a Bill C-31 change.
- Delayed baggage on domestic flights: CTA says the deadline is in the airline's tariff, not 21 days.
- The airline's 30 days is to pay or explain why not.
- Bill C-31 fine increase: the bill replaces the Act's current $250,000 cap with $1,000,000 (the LOP summary says $25,000).
