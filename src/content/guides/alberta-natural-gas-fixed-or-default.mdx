<QuickAnswer>
- If you never signed a gas contract, you're on Alberta's **default gas rate** (the Default Rate Tariff). It changes every month and has **no contract and no exit fee**.
- Alberta's market watchdog found that in 2024 **the default gas rate was lower than competitive fixed and variable offers**. Recent fixed offers have been well above the default rate.
- Most of a gas bill is **delivery and fixed charges** that are the same whichever retailer you pick. Switching only changes the energy part.
- If you do sign a contract, you have <Fact id="energy-contract-cooling-off" /> to cancel without penalty.
</QuickAnswer>

## How your gas bill works

Your bill has two kinds of charges:

1. **Energy:** the price of the gas itself, in dollars per gigajoule (GJ). This is the only part a retailer sets.
2. **Delivery and fees:** transmission, distribution, daily fixed charges, your city's franchise fee and riders. These are regulated and **stay the same whichever retailer you choose**.

When gas prices are low, as they have been since 2024, the energy part is a fairly small share of the bill. That's why switching retailer often saves less than people expect.

## The default rate vs a fixed contract

| | Default rate | Fixed contract |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Changes monthly, approved by the Alberta Utilities Commission | Set for the contract term (up to 5 years) |
| Contract | None | Yes |
| Exit fee | None | Depends on the plan. Alberta has no cap, so check before signing |
| Protection from price spikes | Alberta's <Official href="https://www.alberta.ca/natural-gas-rebate-program" by="Government of Alberta">Natural Gas Rebate Program</Official> automatically rebates prices above <Fact id="gas-rebate-ceiling" /> | Yes, for the term |

For yearly averages: the default rate averaged $6.09 per GJ in 2022, when locking in looked smart, but $3.18 in 2023 and $1.69 in 2024 (Market Surveillance Administrator). Recent monthly rates are on the <Official href="https://ucahelps.alberta.ca/utility-choices/current-rates-and-pricing/default-rates/" by="Government of Alberta">UCA default rates page</Official>.

**When a fixed rate can make sense:** if you'd struggle with a winter bill if prices spiked, and you value a predictable energy price over the chance of paying less. That's a fair choice, just know you're usually paying extra for it.

## Watch out for "dual fuel" bundles

Retailers often offer electricity and gas together, with one bill and a sign-up credit. The catch is that the gas part is usually a fixed price. You can choose a competitive electricity plan and **stay on the default rate for gas**. Price each one separately in the <Official href="https://ucahelps.alberta.ca/cost-comparison-tool/" by="Government of Alberta">UCA Cost Comparison Tool</Official> before you bundle.

## Your rights if you sign a contract

- **Cooling-off:** you can cancel a written or online energy contract within <Fact id="energy-contract-cooling-off" nosource /> without cost or penalty. If you signed over the phone, you can also cancel without penalty within 60 days of your first bill.
- **Exit fees must be disclosed** before you sign. Ask: "What does it cost to leave, and when?"
- **Automatic renewals** can only be for up to a year, and your retailer must warn you before your contract ends.

<Warning label="A salesperson at your door">
Selling energy contracts, furnaces, water heaters, windows and similar products door-to-door without an invitation has been **banned in Alberta** <Fact id="energy-door-to-door-ban" />. If someone comes to your door selling gas or electricity, don't sign anything. Report it to Alberta's consumer protection line at **1-877-427-4088**.
</Warning>

## Lower your gas use this winter

- **Turn the heat down when you're asleep or out.** Natural Resources Canada says lowering the thermostat by 1°C for 8 hours saves about 2% of heating energy. A programmable thermostat does it for you. Don't go below 17°C.
- **Seal draughts.** Air sealing around doors, windows and the attic is the most effective home efficiency fix.
- **Change your furnace filter** about every three months in the heating season so your furnace runs as designed.

At today's low gas prices, these save tens of dollars a year rather than hundreds. They're still worth doing, and they matter more if prices rise.

## Behind on your gas bill?

- Your gas **can't be disconnected for non-payment over the winter** (from November 1 until mid-April). The debt doesn't go away, so call your retailer now and ask for a payment plan.
- If you've already been disconnected, the <Official href="https://www.auc.ab.ca/winter-utility-reconnection-program/" by="Alberta Utilities Commission">Winter Utility Reconnection Program</Official> can help get you reconnected. Call the Utilities Consumer Advocate at **310-4822**.

<HonestTake>
For most Albertans right now, the simplest answer is the right one: stay on the default gas rate, compare electricity separately, and say no to anyone selling contracts at your door. Locking in is insurance against a price spike, and lately that insurance has been expensive. If you value certainty that much, fine, but go in knowing what it costs.
</HonestTake>
