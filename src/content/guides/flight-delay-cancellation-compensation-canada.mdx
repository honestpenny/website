<QuickAnswer>
- If your flight to, from or within Canada is delayed or cancelled for a reason **within the airline's control** (and not needed for safety), and you arrive **3 hours or more late** at your final destination, a large airline owes you <Fact id="appr-delay-comp-large" />, and a small airline <Fact id="appr-delay-comp-small" />. You must have been told 14 days or less before departure.
- **Claim in writing within one year.** The airline has 30 days to pay or explain why not.
- Whatever the cause, if your flight is cancelled or delayed 3 hours or more, the airline must **rebook you**, and offer a **refund** if the rebooking doesn't work for you.
- If the airline says no, you can complain to the **Canadian Transportation Agency (CTA)**, but expect a long wait.
</QuickAnswer>

## First: whose fault was it?

The rules sort every delay and cancellation into three kinds. What you're owed depends on which one it is:

| Kind | Examples | Cash compensation? | Rebooking or refund | Meals and hotel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Within the airline's control** | Staffing schedules, choice of aircraft, planned maintenance | **Yes** | Yes | Yes |
| **Within its control but needed for safety** | A mechanical problem that affects safety (but not one found during scheduled maintenance, which counts as within the airline's control) | No | Yes | Yes |
| **Outside its control** | Weather that makes flying unsafe, air traffic control, medical emergencies, bird strikes, some strikes, a defect flagged by the manufacturer or regulators | No | Yes | No |

The airline must tell you the reason and give updates every 30 minutes. If it calls something "outside our control", ask exactly what. If you complain to the CTA, the law presumes the disruption was within the airline's control (and not for safety) unless the airline proves otherwise.

## Compensation: how much, and how to get it

Delay is measured by when you **arrive at your final destination** on your original ticket (when a door opens after landing), not when you leave.

| Arrival delay | Large airline | Small airline |
|---|---|---|
| 3 to under 6 hours | $400 | $125 |
| 6 to under 9 hours | $700 | $250 |
| 9 hours or more | $1,000 | $500 |

A "large" airline carried 2 million or more passengers worldwide in each of the past two years. Check the CTA's <Official href="https://protection-passager-passenger.otc-cta.gc.ca/en/when-an-issue-happens/large-and-small-airlines" by="CTA">large and small airlines</Official> page.

**How to claim:**

1. Write to the airline (most have an online form) **before the first anniversary** of the flight. Include your booking reference, flight number, date, the delay and what you're asking for.
2. The airline must pay or explain why not **within 30 days**.
3. Compensation must be **money**, unless you agree in writing to something else worth more that doesn't expire. You don't have to accept a voucher.
4. If you took a refund instead of travelling, you're still owed at least $400 from a large airline or $125 from a small one, if the cause was within its control.

## Rebooking and refunds

- **Within the airline's control:** a large airline must rebook you on its own or a partner airline's next flight within 9 hours, and if it can't, on another airline within 48 hours. A small airline must rebook you on its own or a partner's next available flight. If the new plan doesn't suit you, you can take a refund.
- **Outside its control:** the airline must rebook you within 48 hours. If it can't, you can choose a **refund** or another rebooking.
- **Refunds** go back to your original payment method within 30 days. A voucher only counts if you agree to it in writing and it doesn't expire.

## Meals, drinks and a hotel

For disruptions within the airline's control (including safety ones), and if you were told less than 12 hours before departure:

- After a **2-hour** wait, the airline must offer food and drink and a way to communicate.
- If you're stuck **overnight**, it must offer a hotel and transport to and from it.

Keep receipts for anything you have to buy yourself.

## Bumped from your flight (denied boarding)

If the airline won't let you board an overbooked flight for reasons within its control, it owes you <Fact id="appr-denied-boarding" /> depending on how late you arrive (under 6 hours, 6 to 9 hours, 9 hours or more). It must pay within **48 hours**. The amounts are the same for large and small airlines.

## Stuck on the plane (tarmac delays)

At a Canadian airport, after **3 hours** on the tarmac the airline must offer to let you off, unless take-off is likely within 3 hours 45 minutes and it can keep looking after you, or it isn't safe or possible to let you off. During any tarmac delay it must provide working toilets, proper ventilation and heating or cooling, food and drink, and a way to contact people outside if feasible.

## Lost, damaged or delayed bags

- Report it **at the airport** before you leave if you can, and get a report number.
- Claim in writing within **7 days** for a damaged bag. For a delayed bag, claim within **21 days** on international trips; on domestic trips the airline's own rules (its "tariff") set the deadline, so check them.
- A bag counts as **lost after 21 days**, and the airline must refund any bag fee.
- For flights since December 28, 2024, the liability limit is about $2,780 per passenger (1,519 special drawing rights), according to the CTA.

## If the airline says no

1. Write to the airline and give it **30 days** to respond.
2. Then file a complaint on the <Official href="https://protection-passager-passenger.otc-cta.gc.ca/en/air-travel-complaints" by="CTA">CTA's online form</Official>.
3. **Be ready to wait.** The CTA says it may take more than 24 months before your complaint is reviewed, and Transport Canada said in May 2026 that the backlog was more than 97,000 complaints.
4. Paid with a credit card? Ask your card issuer whether a **chargeback** or your card's travel insurance can help. Rules vary by card.

<Warning label="Changes are coming, but they're not law yet">
As introduced, **Bill C-31** would move air complaints from the CTA to the Minister of Transport, bring in a contractor to clear the backlog, make each airline sign up with an approved outside complaint-resolution service, give airlines 30 days to pay what they're ordered to, and raise maximum fines to $1 million. As of November 24, 2026 it was at third reading in the House of Commons and still had to pass the Senate. Its final wording may change before it becomes law. Until it does, the process above is the one that applies.
</Warning>

## Flying from Europe?

The EU's own rules (EU261) cover flights **leaving** the EU on any airline, and flights **into** the EU on an EU airline. Compensation is €250, €400 or €600 depending on distance (halved for some long flights that arrive 3 to 4 hours late). See <Official href="https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm" by="EU">Your Europe: air passenger rights</Official>.

<HonestTake>
Most people never claim, and airlines know it. If your flight was 3 or more hours late and the reason sounds like the airline's own doing (crew, scheduling, overbooking, routine maintenance), claim in writing the same week, ask for cash not vouchers, and keep every receipt and message. If they refuse, file with the CTA anyway. The wait is long, but nothing happens if you don't file.
</HonestTake>

This is general information, not legal advice. The airline's own rules (its tariff) and the regulations are the final word.
