## The 2026 tax brackets

You pay federal tax and Alberta tax. Each applies its rates to slices of your **taxable income** (income after deductions like RRSP contributions).

**Federal, 2026**

| Taxable income | Rate on that slice |
|---|---|
| Up to $58,523 | 14% |
| $58,523 to $117,045 | 20.5% |
| $117,045 to $181,440 | 26% |
| $181,440 to $258,482 | 29% |
| Over $258,482 | 33% |

**Alberta, 2026**

| Taxable income | Rate on that slice |
|---|---|
| Up to $61,200 | 8% |
| $61,200 to $154,259 | 10% |
| $154,259 to $185,111 | 12% |
| $185,111 to $246,813 | 13% |
| $246,813 to $370,220 | 14% |
| Over $370,220 | 15% |

You also don't pay tax on roughly the first $16,452 (federal) and $22,769 (Alberta) of income, thanks to the **basic personal amount** each government gives everyone.

## Your combined rate as an Albertan

Add the two together and you get your **marginal rate**: the tax on your next dollar. These are our calculations from the official brackets above.

| Taxable income, 2026 | Combined marginal rate* |
|---|---|
| Up to $58,523 | 22% |
| $58,523 to $61,200 | 28.5% |
| $61,200 to $117,045 | 30.5% |
| $117,045 to $154,259 | 36% |
| $154,259 to $181,440 | 38% |
| $181,440 to $185,111 | 41% |
| $185,111 to $246,813 | 42% |
| $246,813 to $258,482 | 43% |
| $258,482 to $370,220 | 47% |
| Over $370,220 | 48% |

*Between $181,440 and $258,482, add about 0.3 percentage points, because the federal basic personal amount shrinks over that range.

## Four things people get wrong

1. **"A raise could push me into a higher bracket and I'll take home less."** Not because of tax brackets. Only the dollars above the line are taxed at the higher rate. (Income-tested benefits like the Canada Child Benefit can shrink as income rises, so families on benefits should check the full picture.)
2. **"My refund is a bonus."** A refund means too much tax came off your pay during the year. It's your own money coming back.
3. **"An RRSP saves the same for everyone."** An RRSP deduction saves tax at **your marginal rate**. At $70,000 of income, a $5,000 contribution cuts your tax by about $1,525 (30.5%). On a low income it may save only 22%, or nothing, and the money is taxed when you take it out. That's why a TFSA is often better on a modest income.
4. **"My average rate and my marginal rate are the same."** At $70,000 your average rate is about 17%, but your marginal rate is 30.5%.

## What's coming for 2027

Tax brackets rise with inflation each year. The CRA's payroll formulas for January 2027 show federal brackets rising 2.6% and Alberta's rising 2.0%. For example, the lowest federal bracket will run to $60,044 and Alberta's 8% bracket to $62,424. See the full tables in our <a href="/guides/alberta-income-tax-brackets-2027/">2027 tax brackets guide</a>.

<Warning label="Don't miss the deadline">
If you owe tax and file late, the CRA adds a late-filing penalty (5% of what you owe, plus 1% for each full month late, up to 12 months) on top of interest. So even if you can't pay it all, **file on time**. And file even if you had little or no income, or you could miss benefits you're owed.
</Warning>

<HonestTake>
Brackets scare people out of raises, overtime and side income for no reason. Income tax alone never takes more than about half of an extra dollar in Alberta. What's worth knowing is your marginal rate, because it tells you what an RRSP contribution really saves you and whether a TFSA is the better home for your savings.
</HonestTake>
