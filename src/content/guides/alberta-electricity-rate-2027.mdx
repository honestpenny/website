<QuickAnswer>
- If you never chose a power plan, you're probably on Alberta's default rate, the **Rate of Last Resort (RoLR)**. Your bill will say "Rate of Last Resort" or "RoLR".
- It's been fixed at **about 12 cents per kWh** since January 2025, and that ends on **December 31, 2026**. A new rate starts on January 1, 2027 and normally **can't rise more than 10%**.
- **You can leave the RoLR at any time with no penalty.**
- Compare offers using the government's free <Official href="https://ucahelps.alberta.ca/cost-comparison-tool/" by="Government of Alberta">Utilities Consumer Advocate (UCA) Cost Comparison Tool</Official>, and compare the **total** cost, not just the cents-per-kWh headline.
</QuickAnswer>

## What the default rate is

Since January 1, 2025, Albertans who don't sign a contract with a retailer are on the Rate of Last Resort. It replaced the old Regulated Rate Option. It's set for two years at a time and approved by the Alberta Utilities Commission.

The rates for 2025 and 2026, energy charge only:

| Area | Provider | Rate until 31 Dec 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Calgary and other ENMAX areas | ENMAX | <Fact id="rolr-enmax-2025-2026" /> |
| Edmonton and FortisAlberta areas | EPCOR | <Fact id="rolr-epcor-2025-2026" /> |
| Direct Energy Regulated Services areas | Direct Energy | <Fact id="rolr-direct-energy-2025-2026" /> |

These are energy charges only. Your bill also has delivery charges (transmission and distribution), which are regulated and are **the same whichever retailer you choose**.

Natural gas is different. Alberta's default gas rate still changes every month.

## What happens on January 1, 2027

A new two-year default rate begins. A change from the current rate **cannot normally exceed 10%**, so in the ENMAX area the most it would normally reach is about 13.3¢ per kWh.

As of November 24, 2026 we couldn't find an approved 2027 rate. Alberta's Market Surveillance Administrator has **forecast** about 12.82¢ per kWh for the ENMAX area. That's a forecast, not the approved rate. Providers must file their 2027–2028 rates at least 30 days before January 1, 2027. We'll update this guide as soon as the approved rates are published.

## Should you switch?

Ask three questions.

1. **Is the offer's rate lower than what you'll pay on the default rate?** A fixed offer close to or below 12¢ is worth a look. Above about 13.3¢, it's very unlikely to beat the new default rate.
2. **What else does it charge?** Some retailers add monthly admin or service fees. Small fees can wipe out a lower rate.
3. **What does it cost to leave?** Ask about exit or cancellation fees before you sign. Some plans have none.

## How to compare properly

1. Find your monthly use in kWh on a recent bill. Winter and summer can differ a lot.
2. Use the <Official href="https://ucahelps.alberta.ca/cost-comparison-tool/" by="Government of Alberta">Utilities Consumer Advocate (UCA) Cost Comparison Tool</Official>. It includes the delivery charges for where you live.
3. Check whether a fixed rate is really fixed for the whole term, and whether you can move between fixed and variable.
4. Before switching, ask your current retailer whether you need to give notice or pay an exit fee.
5. After switching, expect a final bill from your old retailer. If nothing arrives from the new one within about a month, call them.

<Warning label="Door-to-door and phone sellers">
A fixed rate isn't the same as a fixed bill. Your bill still goes up and down with how much power you use. Don't sign at the door. Ask for the offer in writing and check it in the UCA tool first.
</Warning>

<HonestTake>
For most people the default rate has been a fair deal since 2025, so there's no need to panic before January. But "fair" isn't "cheapest". Take ten minutes in December: find your kWh, run the UCA tool, and only switch if the saving is real after fees and you can leave without a penalty.
</HonestTake>

## If something goes wrong

If you can't sort out a billing or contract problem with your retailer, the Utilities Consumer Advocate's free mediation team can help. Call **310-4822** in Alberta (option 3) or email ucahelps@gov.ab.ca.
