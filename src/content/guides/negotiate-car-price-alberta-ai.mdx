<QuickAnswer>
- **In Alberta, a dealer's advertised price must be the full price**, except GST and financing costs. AMVIC: the only mandatory fee that can be added is the 5% GST.
- **There's no cooling-off period** once you sign for a car. Do your research and checks first, and don't pay a deposit just to test drive or negotiate.
- **Know your number before you walk in:** a fair price from real comparable listings, your total budget, and your own loan pre-approval.
- **AI is great for the legwork**: summarising listings you paste in, doing the maths, drafting offers. It can be confidently wrong, so check its numbers against the listings, and never paste in your SIN, licence or credit details.
</QuickAnswer>

## Your rights when you buy from an Alberta dealer

- **All-in pricing:** the advertised price must include freight, pre-delivery inspection, administration fees, levies and any products already installed (like nitrogen in the tires or VIN etching). Only GST and financing costs can be added. If a dealer adds a fee to an advertised price, ask them to remove it.
- **No forced add-ons:** AMVIC says no consumer should be forced to agree to extra fees, equipment or services they don't want. You can say no, or walk away.
- **Everything in writing:** the bill of sale must list every fee and extra, the trade-in value, any loan amount rolled in, and the credit details. Promises made out loud should be written into the contract.
- **Used vehicles:** a licensed dealer must give you a **mechanical fitness assessment** before you sign. It isn't a "pass": it says what does and doesn't meet the standard, and a car can still be sold with problems listed. The dealer must also tell you in writing about history like flood, fire or salvage, past rental, taxi or police use, collision or other damage that cost more than $3,000 to repair, and whether it was registered outside Alberta just before the dealer got it.
- **Liens:** if there's a known loan on the car, the dealer must pay it off within 7 days of the sale.
- **Check the dealer is licensed** on AMVIC's website. If a dealer that was AMVIC-licensed when you bought later goes bankrupt, into receivership or loses its licence, AMVIC's compensation fund may cover an eligible loss up to <Fact id="amvic-comp-fund-max" /> (for 2026 transactions). Claim within 3 years of the sale.

<Warning label="Coming in 2027: an Alberta lemon law">
Bill 34 passed third reading on November 18, 2026. Once it's proclaimed (no date has been set yet), it adds a vehicle lemon law covering the first 3 years from the vehicle's first sale or lease or 60,000 km (whichever comes first), for warranty defects that keep failing repair or keep the vehicle in the shop for more than 30 days, plus the right to freeze your credit file. **It isn't in force yet**, so today's rules still apply.
</Warning>

<Warning label="There's no cooling-off period">
AMVIC is clear: when you buy a vehicle, there's no "cooling off" period. If a dealer agrees to let you cancel, it may keep your deposit. Get in writing whether any deposit is refundable before you pay it.
</Warning>

## Step 1: Work out what you can really afford

Count the whole cost of owning the car, not just the payment:

- **The car:** price plus 5% GST from a dealer.
- **Insurance:** get a quote for the exact car **before** you buy. Alberta's Care-First system starts January 1, 2027. See our <a href="/guides/alberta-car-home-insurance/">car insurance guide</a>.
- **Registration:** a one-year renewal online starts at <Fact id="ab-vehicle-registration-1yr" />. Electric vehicles also pay an Alberta EV tax at registration (from $200 a year).
- **Fuel or charging, maintenance, tires** (including winter tires), and **depreciation**: FCAC notes a new car can lose about a quarter of its value in the first year.

Then get a **loan pre-approval** from your own bank or credit union, so you know your rate before the dealer offers theirs.

## Step 2: Find a fair price (this is where AI helps)

Collect 5 to 10 **real listings** for the same model, year range, trim and similar kilometres, from Alberta if you can. Copy each listing's price, year, kilometres, trim and location.

Then ask an AI assistant to do the legwork. For example:

> "Here are 8 listings for a 2022 [model] [trim] in Alberta, with price, km and location. Put them in a table, work out the median and the range, adjust roughly for kilometres, and tell me which listings look high or low and why. Then suggest a fair offer range and a starting offer."

AI is good at:

- Turning messy listings into a clean comparison table
- Working out the median, the spread and a price per kilometre
- Building a total-cost-of-ownership table from your numbers
- Drafting a polite, firm offer email
- Role-playing the dealer so you can practise saying no
- Reading a draft bill of sale (with your name, address and ID number removed) and listing every fee and add-on so you can question them

<Warning label="Check the AI's work">
AI tools can be **confidently wrong**, including inventing prices or rules. Always check its numbers against the listings you gave it, and check any rule against AMVIC or the Government of Alberta. The Privacy Commissioner of Canada advises not sharing sensitive or identifiable personal information with chatbots: never paste in your SIN, driver's licence, bank details or credit report. If a dealer's own chatbot promises you something, get it written into the bill of sale.
</Warning>

## Step 3: Negotiate

- **Negotiate the total price, not the monthly payment.** A lower payment can just mean a longer loan.
- **Make your offer in writing** (email works) based on your comparables: "Similar cars are listed between $X and $Y. I can buy today at $Z, all-in before GST."
- **Ask for the out-the-door price**, itemised, before you agree.
- **Treat the trade-in, financing and add-ons as separate conversations**, so a good number in one doesn't hide a bad one in another. (This is common advice, not a rule.)
- **Be ready to walk away.** It's your strongest tool.
- **Sale banners and "employee pricing" aren't proof of a good deal.** Compare against real listings.

## Step 4: Check the financing and the extras

- The dealer **doesn't have to offer you the lowest interest rate**, FCAC warns. Compare with your pre-approval.
- Alberta law requires a **disclosure statement** with the annual percentage rate (APR) and total cost of credit **before** you sign, and you can pay off a car **loan** (not a lease) early **without a penalty**.
- Be careful with **long loans** (72 months or more) and **negative equity** (rolling an old loan into a new one): you can end up owing more than the car is worth for years.
- **Loan insurance, extended warranties and protection packages are optional.** Ask for the price of each separately, and say no to anything you don't want.
- Interest above 35% APR is a criminal rate in Canada.

## Buying privately

- **No GST** is generally charged on a private sale of a used car, and Alberta has no provincial sales tax.
- **Search for liens** with the car's VIN through an Alberta registry agent (a small government fee plus the agent's charge), so you don't inherit someone else's loan.
- **Get your own mechanical inspection.** Private sellers don't have to give you one, and AMVIC doesn't cover private sales.
- Watch for **curbsiders**: people selling several cars while posing as private sellers. If something feels off, walk away.

## Trading in

At a GST-registered dealer, GST is generally charged on the price **after** your trade-in is subtracted, which saves you 5% of the trade-in value (for example $500 on a $10,000 trade-in) compared with selling privately. Compare both: get a written trade-in value, and check what similar cars sell for privately.

<HonestTake>
The two things that save the most money are boring: knowing a fair price from real listings, and arranging your own financing before you walk in. AI is a brilliant assistant for both, as long as you check its numbers and keep your personal details out of it. And remember there's no cooling-off period: the research has to happen before you sign, not after.
</HonestTake>
