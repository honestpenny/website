<QuickAnswer>
- Since <Fact id="telecom-fee-ban-start" />, providers can't charge you **activation, set-up or plan-change fees**. Changing plans or providers is cheaper than it used to be.
- The biggest saving is often a **cheaper brand owned by the same company**: TELUS owns Koodo and Public Mobile; Rogers owns Fido and chatr; Bell owns Virgin Plus and Lucky Mobile.
- Before you leave, **check what you still owe on your phone.** Device balances are still due when you cancel.
- Low-income families and seniors may qualify for internet at <Fact id="connecting-families-plans" /> through the federal Connecting Families program.
</QuickAnswer>

## Step 1: Find out what you're really paying

Look at your last bill and write down:

- The monthly price, and **when any promo price ends**. Promo prices often jump after a year or two.
- How much data you actually use. Your provider's app shows the last few months. Many people pay for far more than they use.
- What you still owe on your phone (the "device balance") and how many months are left.

## Step 2: Compare the cheaper brands

The big three companies each own cheaper brands:

| Company | Main brand | Cheaper brands |
|---|---|---|
| TELUS | TELUS | Koodo, Public Mobile |
| Rogers | Rogers | Fido, chatr |
| Bell | Bell | Virgin Plus, Lucky Mobile |

Freedom Mobile, owned by Vidéotron, is the main competitor to the big three.

Coverage in Alberta varies, especially outside cities. Check each provider's coverage map for where you live and work before you switch.

**Bring your own phone.** If your phone is paid off, a plan without a phone ("bring your own device") is usually much cheaper. Phones from Canadian providers now come unlocked, and your provider must unlock an older phone for free if you ask.

## Step 3: Ask your current provider to match it

Once you have a real offer in hand, call your provider and ask for the "loyalty" or "retention" department:

> "I've been a customer for [X] years. I can get [plan] from [brand] for $[price] a month. I'd rather stay. Can you match it or do better? If not, I'd like to cancel."

Be polite, be ready to actually leave, and **get any new deal confirmed in writing**. Because the fees for changing plans are gone, saying "I'll leave" is a real option.

## Step 4: Switch, carefully

1. Keep your number: start service with the new provider **first**, and ask it to transfer your number. Don't cancel the old account yourself before the transfer finishes.
2. **Your trial period:** if your new contract has an early cancellation fee, the Wireless Code gives you at least 15 days to try it and cancel (30 days, with double the usage, for people who identify as having a disability). You must stay under the provider's usage limit, which can't be less than half your monthly allowance, and return the phone in near-new condition with its original packaging.
3. Check your first bill from both providers.

<Warning label="Fees that are still allowed">
The fee ban doesn't cover everything. You can still be charged for: **what's left on a financed phone**, equipment you don't return (like a modem), a technician visit to your home, early cancellation on some fixed-term internet contracts, and reconnection after missed payments. Some providers have also added new SIM or set-up charges, which the CRTC is reviewing. Ask for them to be waived.
</Warning>

## Your rights on your cellphone bill

- **Bill-shock caps:** unless the account holder agrees to more, your provider must suspend extra data charges once they reach <Fact id="wireless-data-overage-cap" /> in a month, and **data** roaming charges at <Fact id="wireless-roaming-cap" />.
- **Cancel any time:** cancellation takes effect the day your provider gets your notice. If your phone was discounted with a contract, the cancellation fee can't be more than the discount and drops to $0 by 24 months.
- **Unlocking is free.**

## Cheaper home internet

- **Ask for the same deal new customers get.** Internet promos are aimed at new customers, and many providers will match them to keep you.
- **Check smaller providers.** Independent internet providers can now rent access to the big companies' fibre in more areas, so it's worth checking who serves your address.
- **Low-income help:** if your family gets the maximum Canada Child Benefit, or you're a senior getting at least 80% of the maximum Guaranteed Income Supplement, you may qualify for <Official href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/programs-and-initiatives/connecting-families-initiative/eligibility" by="Government of Canada">Connecting Families</Official> internet at <Fact id="connecting-families-plans" nosource />. You need an invitation letter from the government to sign up.
- **Rural Alberta:** if your home can't get high-speed internet, the <Official href="https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-satellite-rebate-program" by="Government of Alberta">Alberta Satellite Rebate</Official> covers <Fact id="alberta-satellite-rebate" /> of satellite equipment and installation. Buy and apply by **March 31, 2027**.

<HonestTake>
Most Albertans can save money on their phone in under an hour: check your data use, compare the cheaper brands, and either switch or make your provider match it. The one thing that catches people is the device balance. If you still owe $600 on your phone, you'll owe it whether you stay or go, so do the sums first.
</HonestTake>

## If something goes wrong

Complain to your provider first, in writing, and keep notes of calls. If it isn't fixed, the <Official href="https://www.ccts-cprst.ca/for-consumers/telecom-complaints/complaints-process-explained/" by="free, independent">Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS)</Official> handles complaints about cellphone, internet and TV services for free, such as billing errors, contract disputes and service problems.
