<QuickAnswer>
- Your property tax is based on your **assessment**: what the city says your home was worth on **July 1 of the previous year**.
- If the city has your home's details wrong, or values it above similar homes nearby, you can ask for a fix. **Talking to the assessor is free.**
- If that doesn't work, you can file a formal complaint with your city's Assessment Review Board. For a house or condo in Calgary that cost **$50 in 2026, or $40 if received before January 31**, and the fee is refunded if you win.
- The window is short, about **60 to 70 days** from when the notice is mailed in January. Your exact deadline is printed on your notice.
</QuickAnswer>

## What an assessment is, and why it matters

Every January, Alberta cities mail an assessment notice. It's the city's estimate of your property's market value as of July 1 of the year before. Your tax bill later in the spring is worked out from that number.

The assessment doesn't set your tax rate. Council does that. But if your assessment is too high compared with similar homes, you pay more than your fair share. A lower assessment means a lower bill.

## Step 1: Check the facts about your home

Most fixable errors are simple facts, not opinions about value. Look up your property online and check:

- Square footage and number of storeys
- Whether the basement is listed as finished (and whether it really is)
- Lot size, garage, and any extras listed
- Condition problems the city may not know about, such as foundation cracks or water damage

In Calgary, use <Official href="https://mytax.calgary.ca/" by="City of Calgary">myTax</Official>. In Edmonton, use <Official href="https://myproperty.edmonton.ca/" by="City of Edmonton">MyProperty</Official>. Other Alberta towns have their own lookups; your notice says where.

## Step 2: Compare with similar homes nearby

Find three to five homes like yours: same area, similar size, age and style. Compare:

- Their assessments (Calgary's myTax has property, sales and map searches, plus market trend reports)
- What similar homes actually sold for **around July 1 of last year**, because that's the date your value is based on

If similar homes are assessed noticeably lower, or sold for less than your assessment, write it down. For a formal complaint, a link isn't enough: Calgary's board says links aren't treated as evidence. Save each page as a PDF or image.

## Step 3: Call the assessor first. It's free

Before you file anything, phone **311** (Calgary or Edmonton) or use the city's online form during the review period. Explain what's wrong and send your evidence.

If the city agrees, it can correct your assessment without a formal complaint. This is the quickest, cheapest way to a lower bill, and many questions are settled here.

## Step 4: File a formal complaint if you still disagree

If the call doesn't fix it, file with your local **Assessment Review Board (ARB)** before the deadline on your notice. In Calgary you can file online, in person or by mail. The board doesn't accept complaints by email or fax.

| | Calgary (2026 fees) |
|---|---|
| House, condo, or up to 3 dwellings; farmland | **$50**, or **$40** if received before January 31 |
| 4+ dwellings or non-residential | **$650** |
| Fee refunded if | the board rules in your favour, or you reach an agreement with the City |

Edmonton's fee for a house or condo is $50 under its <Official href="https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/documents/bl18308.pdf" by="City of Edmonton">Assessment Review Boards Bylaw</Official> (as of 2026). Check your 2027 notice.

What to expect:

1. **File** before the deadline, with the correct fee. A wrong or partial fee can make your complaint invalid.
2. **Disclose your evidence** by the board's dates. In Calgary you upload it through the ePortal (up to 250 MB). Evidence that isn't disclosed on time won't be heard.
3. **Attend the hearing.** Postponements are only granted for exceptional reasons.
4. **Get a written decision.**

You can withdraw your complaint in writing, for any reason, at least three days before the hearing, for example if the city agrees to a fix.

<HonestTake>
Most people never check their assessment, and cities only fix what they're told about. If your basement is listed as finished when it isn't, or your square footage is wrong, that's worth a phone call every time. Arguing about value is harder: bring real comparable homes, not a feeling that "it seems high". And remember a lower assessment only lowers *your share* of the tax. If every value in the city goes up, the rate usually comes down.
</HonestTake>

## If you're 65 or older and struggling to pay

Alberta's **Seniors Property Tax Deferral Program** lets homeowners aged 65+ with at least 25% equity defer their property tax as a low-interest loan from the province. **There's no income test.** Interest was <Fact id="seniors-tax-deferral-rate" /> simple when we checked (November 2026), and the loan is repaid when you sell the home, stop owning it or living in it, or on death (a surviving spouse aged 55+ may be able to continue it). Apply at least 30 days before your city's property tax deadline. That deadline is usually June 30, so apply by about May 31. Details: <Official href="https://www.alberta.ca/seniors-property-tax-deferral-program" by="Government of Alberta">Seniors Property Tax Deferral Program</Official>.

## Living outside Calgary or Edmonton?

Every Alberta municipality follows the same provincial rules under the Municipal Government Act: notices in the new year, a deadline printed on the notice (usually 60 days), and a local or regional Assessment Review Board. Fees and portals differ, so read your notice.
