- Shop around anyway. Use the rate board's <Official href="https://www.airbfordrivers.ca/resources/" by="Automobile Insurance Rate Board">tools and resources</Official>, then get quotes from a broker and at least one direct insurer.

## What Care-First changes

Today, after a crash you get limited accident benefits from your own insurer, and if someone else caused it you can sue them, with their insurance paying. From <Fact id="care-first-start" nosource />, Alberta moves to a "care first" system:

- **You claim from your own insurer**, no matter who was at fault.
- **Medical and rehab care** has no lifetime limit, instead of today's cap of $50,000 over two years.
- **Income replacement** is much higher and can last until age 65.
- **Suing the other driver is limited** to specific situations, such as the other driver being convicted of certain driving offences.

Which system applies depends on **the date of the collision**, not when you bought your policy.

## Who pays less, and who pays more

The Automobile Insurance Rate Board looked at insurers' approved prices for the new system:

| Who | Change in premium |
|---|---|
| Average private car | <Fact id="care-first-avg-saving" /> |
| Young male drivers (example profile) | down about $3,094 (30%) |
| Middle-aged drivers (example profiles) | down about $525 (about 14%) |
| Motorcycles | **up** at least 37% |

Most drivers save, but not everyone. Check your own renewal instead of assuming.

## The rate caps

- **2026:** if you're a "good driver" (no at-fault claims in 6 years, and no serious or minor convictions in recent years), your rise is capped at <Fact id="good-driver-cap-2026" />. You can lose the cap if you switch insurers, move, add a driver or change vehicles, so ask before you change anything.
- **From 2027:** an insurer can't raise its average rates more than 5% a year, and a rate change can't push your premium up more than <Fact id="auto-renewal-cap-2027" nosource />. Your own risk changes (claims, tickets, a new car, a new driver) aren't covered by the cap.
- **The "grid" ends:** Alberta's maximum price for basic coverage, which mostly helped newer drivers, ends on January 1, 2027 with no replacement. New and young drivers should shop around carefully.

## Your renewal checklist

1. **Compare this year's total with last year's**, line by line.
2. **Ask which discounts you're getting, and which you're missing.** The rate board lists bundling home and car, **winter tires**, anti-theft devices, and alumni or professional groups. Ask about any others your insurer offers.
3. **Ask what a higher deductible would save.** Only choose one you could pay tomorrow.
4. **Ask the total cost of paying monthly versus once a year.**
5. **Don't cut liability to save a few dollars.** The legal minimum is <Fact id="auto-liability-minimum" />, but a serious crash can cost far more. Most drivers choose $1 million or $2 million.
6. **Get two or three quotes:** one broker (who can quote several insurers) and at least one direct insurer.

## Home, tenant and condo insurance

Hail is a big reason Alberta home insurance has gone up. Calgary's August 2024 hailstorm caused about $2.9 billion in insured damage, according to the Automobile Insurance Rate Board.

- **Check your water coverage.** Overland flooding and sewer backup are often optional add-ons with their own limits. Ask whether you have them.
- **Replacing a roof?** Ask your insurer first whether it gives a discount for impact-resistant roofing or siding.
- **Renters:** tenant insurance covers your belongings and your liability if, say, a leak from your unit damages the one below.
- **Condo owners:** since February 26, 2026, your condo corporation can charge you back the lesser of the repair cost or its insurance deductible, up to <Fact id="condo-deductible-chargeback-max" />, for damage caused by the actions or inaction of you, your tenant or a guest. Make sure your own condo policy covers that **deductible chargeback** amount.

<Warning label="Before you cancel a policy">
Never let car insurance lapse, even for a day: driving uninsured is illegal. Have the new policy confirmed in writing before the old one ends.
</Warning>

<HonestTake>
Care-First is a big change to Alberta car insurance, and for most drivers it should mean a smaller bill. Don't let that make you complacent: the old price ceiling for new drivers is going away, and insurers still price the same driver very differently. Twenty minutes and three quotes at renewal is still the best-paid job most people will do all year.
</HonestTake>

## If you have a complaint

1. Start with your broker or agent, then your insurer's complaints officer or ombudsperson. Ask for a **final position letter** in writing.
2. Then go to the <Official href="https://giocanada.org/how-the-process-works/" by="independent, free">General Insurance OmbudService</Official>. It's free, but it doesn't handle disputes about price.
3. For how an insurer treated you, contact Alberta's <Official href="https://www.alberta.ca/insurance-consumer-complaints" by="Government of Alberta">Superintendent of Insurance</Official>. For problems with an agent, broker or adjuster, contact the Alberta Insurance Council.
