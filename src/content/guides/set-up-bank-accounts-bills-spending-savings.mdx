<QuickAnswer>
- **Three accounts is a simple setup that works for many people:** one for **bills**, one for **everyday spending**, and one or more for **savings**.
- **Automate it on payday:** transfers go out the day you're paid, so saving happens before spending. Canada's Financial Consumer Agency (FCAC) recommends exactly this for emergency funds.
- **Don't pay more than you need to:** a <Fact id="low-cost-account-fee" /> low-cost account, or a free one if you qualify, may be all you need.
- **Your deposits are protected, up to limits:** eligible deposits are insured up to <Fact id="cdic-coverage-per-category" /> per insured category at each CDIC member bank. Investments like stocks, mutual funds and ETFs aren't covered. Most Alberta credit union deposits and all ATB deposits are protected under Alberta's own rules.
</QuickAnswer>

## Why separate accounts work

Behavioural research has found two useful things: saving that happens automatically beats saving that relies on remembering, and money split into separate, labelled pots is more likely to be left alone (that second finding comes from a smaller number of studies). No study has tested the exact "three accounts" setup below, but it puts both ideas to work.

## The three-account setup

| Account | What goes in | What comes out |
|---|---|---|
| **Bills** (chequing) | On payday: your fixed costs plus your monthly set-aside for irregular costs | Rent or mortgage, utilities, phone, insurance, loan payments, all on autopay |
| **Spending** (chequing or card) | On payday: what's left for groceries, gas, fun | Day-to-day spending. When it's low, you slow down. No guilt, no maths |
| **Savings** (high-interest savings or TFSA) | On payday: emergency fund, then goals | Only real emergencies, or the goal it's for |

**Set it up in an afternoon:**

1. Work out your monthly numbers with our <a href="/budget/">budget planner</a>.
2. Have your pay land in the **bills** account.
3. Set **automatic transfers on payday**: the spending amount to the spending account, and the savings amount to savings. These are monthly amounts: if you're paid every two weeks, each payday transfer is the monthly amount × 12 ÷ 26 (about 46%); if you're paid weekly, it's × 12 ÷ 52.
4. Move every bill to **autopay from the bills account**.
5. For irregular costs (registration, insurance, property tax, Christmas), either keep them in the bills account or open a separate savings account just for them.

**Where to keep savings:** a high-interest savings account for the emergency fund, ideally inside a **TFSA** so the interest is tax-free and you can still take it out any time. But money you take out only gets its room back on January 1 of the next year, so putting it back sooner can trigger a 1%-a-month penalty if you have no unused room. See <a href="/guides/tfsa-rrsp-fhsa-compared/">which account to use</a>.

## Couples: joint, separate or both?

There's no single right answer, but there's real research and some cautions:

- **A joint account means shared responsibility.** FCAC says joint holders share responsibility for all transactions, and that using an overdraft can create debt for everyone on the account.
- **Ask what happens if one of you dies.** FCAC suggests asking your bank how the account is treated in that case.
- **What the research found:** in a 2023 US study, 230 engaged or newlywed couples who all started with separate accounts were randomly asked to use a joint account, keep separate accounts, or choose their own way for two years. Couples given a joint account reported their relationship quality holding steady, while the others declined on average. Partly combining accounts didn't show the same effect, though the researchers say more study is needed. The researchers also warn that joint accounts can be used for financial control.

A common middle way: a joint account for shared bills and goals, plus a personal spending account each. Whatever you choose, both partners should be able to see the shared money and have some money of their own.

<Warning label="If a partner controls the money">
Taking your pay, blocking you from accounts or running up debt in your name can be financial abuse. You can get confidential help: call or text 211 Alberta, or see <a href="/get-help/">Get help</a>.
</Warning>

## Cut your bank fees

FCAC's 2026 review found monthly fees at Canada's six biggest banks range from about $4 for basic accounts to $12 to $18 for standard accounts and about $29 to $31 for premium ones. FCAC found many people pay for an account plan that doesn't match how they actually bank.

- **Low-cost accounts:** under a voluntary commitment in force since December 1, 2025, participating banks offer a low-cost account for <Fact id="low-cost-account-fee" /> or less, with at least 18 debit transactions.
- **No-cost accounts** are available at participating banks to young people (18 or under), students, seniors receiving the Guaranteed Income Supplement, people with a Registered Disability Savings Plan, and newcomers in their first year. Some banks extend them further. Ask your bank, or see <Official href="https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/banking/bank-accounts/low-cost-no-cost.html" by="FCAC">low-cost and no-cost accounts</Official>.
- **Bounced payments:** since March 12, 2026, federally regulated banks can't charge more than <Fact id="nsf-fee-cap" /> for a non-sufficient funds (NSF) charge. ATB and Alberta credit unions are provincially regulated, so check their own fees.

Ask your bank to move you to a cheaper account. If they won't, switching takes a form, then updating your pay deposit, government deposits and every autopay. Keep the old account open for a month or two so nothing bounces.

## Is my money safe?

- **Banks:** the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) covers eligible deposits up to <Fact id="cdic-coverage-per-category" /> per insured category at each member institution. The categories are deposits in one name, joint deposits, RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs, RDSPs, RESPs, FHSAs and trust deposits. So eligible deposits in a TFSA and a regular savings account at the same bank are covered separately.
- **Joint accounts:** the same group of owners shares one coverage limit; a different group of owners gets its own.
- **Alberta credit unions:** deposits at credit unions regulated in Alberta are protected in full, including interest, by the <Official href="https://cudgc.ab.ca/" by="Credit Union Deposit Guarantee Corporation">Credit Union Deposit Guarantee Corporation</Official>. Shares and investments like mutual funds aren't covered. A few credit unions are federal (for example Innovation Federal Credit Union), and their deposits fall under CDIC's limits instead, so check which one your credit union uses.
- **ATB Financial:** deposits are backed by the Government of Alberta.

<HonestTake>
Separate accounts and automatic transfers do the hard part for you: saving happens before you see the money, and the spending account tells you when to slow down. Set it up once, check it monthly, and keep your fees as low as your bank allows.
</HonestTake>
